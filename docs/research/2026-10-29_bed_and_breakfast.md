# Research: a bed-and-breakfast inn in R-1 and R-2 (City of Ithaca)

**Purpose:** the research behind the open question on `<district>.use` (R-1a, R-1b, R-2a, R-2b, R-2c): is a bed-and-breakfast inn prohibited there, as the chart's "-" says, or allowed with a special permit, as §325-9B(1)(p) says?\
**For:** anyone checking why Gordium says "needs review" for a bed-and-breakfast inn in R-1 and R-2; architects and the City answering the question.\
**Kept current by:** nobody: a dated record. New research gets its own dated report.

Chapter 325 quotes are from Gordium's stored copy
(`regulations/jurisdictions/new_york/ithaca_city/2_extracted_text/chapter_325_zoning_2026-10.jsonl`); page numbers
are that PDF's pages. Chart quotes are from `zoning_charts_2026-04.pdf`.

**Class** (as in `2026-10-20_architect_questions.md`): **A** = answered by the code's own text; **B** = Gordium's
reading, well sourced, shown to the architect to confirm; **C** = genuinely unclear, an open question.

## 1. The conflict — C

- Chart p.2 line 28: "Bed-and-breakfast inn - - - - - SP SP SP SP §325-10(C)(2)", in the columns R-1A, R-1B, R-2A,
  R-2B, R-2C, R-3A, R-3AA, R-3B, RU: "-" (not permitted) in R-1 and R-2, SP in R-3 and RU.
- §325-9B(1) (p.30–31): "The uses listed under the district regulations in § 325-8, District regulations, which
  require a special permit from the Planning and Development Board, are as follows: ... (p) Bed-and-breakfast inns in
  all districts."

   interpretation and no Board of Zoning Appeals decision on a bed-and-breakfast inn in R-1 or R-2.
3. **Building codes and other cities:** not relevant; the question is which of Ithaca's own two texts governs.

## 3. What each side has

**For a special permit:** §325-9B(1)(p) says "in all districts". On the residential chart (p.2), every other "in all
districts" use (cemeteries, public utility facilities, towers, neighborhood gardens) is SP in every district.

**For the chart:**
- §325-9B(1) introduces its list as "The uses listed under the district regulations in § 325-8": the list describes
  the chart, and §325-8A makes the chart part of the chapter. §325-10A speaks the same way: "certain uses allowed by
  special permit under § 325-8, District regulations".
- The chart's own bed-and-breakfast row cites §325-10(C)(2), the permit's conditions, beside its "-" marks.
- Elsewhere the chart departs from the list's "all districts" both ways: bed-and-breakfast inns are "•" (no permit) in
  the waterfront districts (p.10 line 20) and "-" in CR-1, CR-2, MU-1 and MU-2 (p.12 line 19); cemeteries are "•" in
  MU-1 and MU-2. So "in all districts" in that list reads as loose wording.

**Neither:** §325-9F(1)(c) ("A bed-and-breakfast inn special permit in all residential districts shall be issued for a
five-year period", p.34) and §325-10C(2) (p.35: the conditions "applicable to all special permits for
bed-and-breakfast inns") set the permit's term and conditions; they presume a permit and grant none.

**Which board:** §325-9B(1) and §325-9C name the Planning and Development Board. §325-20's footnote 3 (p.55) speaks
of "the Zoning Board of Appeals ... during consideration of the special permit", but the same footnote is on the home
occupation row (p.54), whose permit is plainly the Planning Board's: a leftover about lowering the parking, not a
second board.

## 4. Why it stays open, and what Gordium does

The text and the chart disagree, each side has support, and no City record settles it. Until the architects or the
City answer, a bed-and-breakfast inn in R-1a, R-1b, R-2a, R-2b and R-2c gets NEEDS REVIEW, saying it may be prohibited
or allowed only with the Planning and Development Board's special permit (before 2026-10-29 it did not comply). The
question is §5.18 of `docs/questions_for_architects.md`.

# New York's Building Code Chapter 9, the parts egress turns on: the plan (2026-11-27)

**Purpose:** which sections of Chapter 9 (fire protection and life safety systems) of the 2025 Building Code of New York State Gordium brings in for egress, what is a rule and what a flag, the building facts they read, where the rules go, and the order of the PRs.\
**For:** engineers extending `new_york_state_building/`; Ben (scope, order); reviewers of each part's PR.\
**Kept current by:** the engineer who drafts a part, in that same branch (the part's status line below).

Follows [Chapter 10's plan](2026-11-23_ny_building_egress_all_occupancies.md) and [Chapter 7's](2026-11-26_ny_building_chapter_7.md). Chapter 10 gives many allowances to a building "equipped throughout with an automatic sprinkler system in accordance with Section 903.3.1.1 or 903.3.1.2" (Tables 1006.2.1, 1017.2 and 1020.2, 1007.1.1, 1005.3's smaller factors), and some to one with an emergency voice/alarm system per 907.5.2.2. Until now Gordium took the sprinkler system as the architect's statement (`sprinkler_system`: none, NFPA 13, 13R or 13D) and never asked whether the law requires one. Chapter 9 says where a system is required (903.2), which system may be used (903.3.1), and where a fire alarm and an emergency voice/alarm system are required (907.2). The same pipeline as Chapters 7 and 10: sources.yaml, extraction, every number read a second time from the page images, labels, rules or flags, coverage.

## Decisions

1. **One rulebook file.** Chapter 9's rules go in `drafted/general.yaml` as their own block in book order: after Chapter 7's block and before Chapter 10's first rule (`general.bc_1004_7_outdoor_areas`), so the Chapter 4 work (before 508), the Chapter 7 work and the Chapter 10 work touch other lines of the file. Pinned by `test_chapter_9_rules_sit_in_book_order_between_chapters_7_and_10` (packages/rule_format/tests/test_ny_building_chapter_9.py); Chapter 7's own order test now accepts Chapter 9's block after it. Labels and text verification entries go in book order too.
2. **A required system is a rule; how it is designed is a flag.** Where 903.2 requires a sprinkler system for the stated groups and the stated system is none, or one 903.3.1 doesn't permit there (a "lesser type": NFPA 13R outside Group R, NFPA 13D outside one- and two-family dwellings, Group R-3, Group R-4 Condition 1 and townhouses), the check fails. It passes only where the stated system is one the law permits for the stated groups. Where a fact it needs isn't stated (the groups, the system) or a permission's condition isn't in the model (903.3.1.2's heights above fire department vehicle access, Group R-4's or Group I-1's Condition, an I-4 day care's exterior doors), it needs review, never a pass. The system's design (Table 903.2.5.2's hazard classes, 13R's corridors and balconies, alternative systems) is a flag.
3. **The groups as facts.** A rule reads the building's one group (`occupancy_group`) and, for a building of two or more groups, whether any stated group is one 903.2 requires a system for whatever its size (new measures from the stated groups: `group_r_present`, `group_h_present`, `group_i1_i2_i3_present`), so a mixed building with a Group R, H or I-1 to I-3 occupancy and no sprinkler system fails. A building of two or more groups with an NFPA 13R or 13D system needs review: the one stated system can't say which portion has which.
4. **903.3.1.2's limits.** An NFPA 13R system is permitted "in Group R occupancies" four stories or fewer above grade plane, with the roof (Group R-2) or the highest floor (other Group R) within a height of the lowest level of fire department vehicle access, and the lowest floor within 30 ft below it. The stories are the architect's (`stories_above_grade_plane`, Revit's Gordium: Stories above grade plane, now also read by the Building Code's rules); the heights aren't in the model. So 13R in Group R needs review, and fails where the stated stories are more than four; 13R outside Group R fails where 903.2 requires a system (Group H, I-2, I-3), and needs review in Group I-1 (903.2.6 exception 1, Condition 1). 903.3.1.2 itself is a flag wherever a 13R system is stated (counted or not: `nfpa_13r_stated`, review round 4) or the system isn't: group 5's false pass (a Group B building with a 13R system given Chapter 10's sprinklered allowances) came from 13R outside Group R. Review round 1 found four Chapter 10 rules still counting it there (1007.1.1's story and space remoteness, 1007.1.2's, Group I-4's two exits, 1006.2.1): a Group B story's exits 0.4 of the diagonal apart passed at one-third. Each now counts a 13R system only in a building stated Group R and needs review between its limits otherwise, and a walk over every rule reading the 13R fact holds that no part passes with 13R outside Group R that doesn't pass with no system (`test_no_rule_passes_on_a_13r_system_outside_group_r`). Round 3: in a building stated Group R alone, a 13R system with more than four stated stories above grade plane is no 903.3.1.2 system at all (`code_scope.building_measures` makes the fact no), so no Chapter 10 allowance counts it and 903.2.8 fails it; its heights stay the architect's statement, as Chapter 10's plan decided (group 4b), and so do the stories while not stated. Round 2: 1007.1.1's and 1007.1.2's thresholds are written so three-valued logic decides them ("ratio at least one-half, or at least one-third with a sprinkler system that counts"), so a 13R system stated with the groups not stated no longer leaves a pair a full diagonal apart needing review.
   **Revised 2026-11-28 (the 13R limits PR, the coordinator's decision under principle 4):** a rule whose condition is "sprinklered per 903.3.1.2" isn't met where 903.3.1.2 doesn't permit 13R in that building, so passing on a stated 13R system whose limits aren't known is a false pass, and the flag beside the row doesn't cure it. The heights stay the architect's statement, but an unstated limit is unknown, never assumed met. `code_scope.building_measures` (`nfpa_13r_counts`) now makes `sprinklered_903_3_1_2` yes only where the building is stated Group R alone, its stories above grade plane are stated four or fewer, and its heights are stated within items 2 and 3 (`building.nfpa_13r_heights_within`, Revit's Gordium: 13R heights within limits); no where a limit is stated broken (more than four stories, the heights not within, a Group R-2 high-rise) or no Group R or I-1 is among the stated groups; not known otherwise (the groups not stated, Group R beside another group, I-1, a limit not stated), so every rule resting on it needs review and asks the first limit not stated, once (`field_of`). This covers every rule reading the fact centrally (the Table 1006.2.1 rows, 1006.3.4's tables, 1007.1.1 and 1007.1.2, Table 1017.2, Table 1020.2, 1020.3, 1009, 1011.2, 1013.2, 1031, 716.2.2.3 and the 903.2 rules). 903.2.8 now passes a 13R system counted that way (a system per 903.3) and reads "a 13R system stated" as `nfpa_13r_stated` where it means stated (903.2.5.1's, 903.2.6's mixed and I-1 reviews). Why not keep the statement: the R-2 one-exit row (Table 1006.3.4(1) note a) passed a six-story R-2 whose model held its lower floors on a 13R system 903.3.1.2 forbids there (the story facts line's review, 2026-11-28).

5. **New buildings.** 903.2 and 907.2 are for new buildings and structures; the check is of a new design (an existing building's alteration is the Existing Building Code's).

**Purpose:** how Gordium's first building-code rules (egress for one- and two-family houses, 2025 Residential Code of New York State) were made, and why each check is there.\
**For:** engineers changing `rule_format/code_numbers.py`, `rule_format/parts.yaml`, `rule_drafting/book.py` or `book_eval.py`; Ben; the architects reading the rules.\
**Kept current by:** the engineer who changes those files, in that PR.

Step 1 brought in the law ([2026-11-12_building_code_text.md](2026-11-12_building_code_text.md)); step 2 made rules repeat per part ([2026-11-12_rules_for_each_part.md](2026-11-12_rules_for_each_part.md)).

## Decisions

1. **Same method as zoning.** One recorded AI call per piece of the law, the answer forced into the rule format, every draft checked by code (exact quotes, valid formulas, worked examples with pass, fail and "none", provenance), retried up to twice, then listed for a person. `python -m rule_drafting draft --state new_york/new_york_state --all --yes`. Drafts and every call's record go to `new_york_state/5_building_code_rules/drafted/` (a state folder's rulebooks are `5_building_code_rules/`, not `5_zoning_rules/`: its law is a building code). Drafts stay draft (approval deferred, 2026-10-19).
2. **What is drafted** (`book.SCOPE`, 25 pieces, all labeled rule): R318.2 exit door; R318.7.1, .2, .5.1, .5.2, .8 stairs (width, headroom, risers 8 1/4 in at most and treads 9 in at least, New York's own numbers, handrails); R319.1 to R319.4.2 escape openings (required, size, height and width, sill, under decks, doors, area wells and ladders); BF103.2 escape openings into a patio cover; R320.2 and R320.4 handrails; R321.1.1 to R321.1.3 guards; R315.2, R315.3, R315.5.2.1, R315.5.2.2 for a space marked a sleeping loft. **Appendix BO** (existing buildings) stays flags in the labels: whether a stair exists, was lawful when built, and the class of the work are the architect's input. Not drafted here: ramps, landings, R318.7.3 and the special stairs, R320.3/.5/.6, R319.4.2.1/.2.2, R319.6 (drafted since, by the same method: [2026-11-14_house_egress_remaining.md](2026-11-14_house_egress_remaining.md)); R319.5, R319.5.1 and R319.7 are labeled flag and stay flags.
3. **Numbers come from the second reading, never the extracted words alone** (`rule_format/code_numbers.py`, used by validation for every rule citing a book with a `text_verification.yaml`, and by the drafter's number binding). Each number a formula uses must be one of the cited piece's second-reading numbers that its quote states, in the measure's unit (8 1/4 inches is 8.25; a feet number followed by an inches number is one length: 6 feet 8 inches is 80 for a measure in inches). Its comparison must fit the second reading's: where the comparison must hold for the formula to hold (the whole check or applies_when, a part of a top "and", a "not" turned round by De Morgan) it must be the law's own (`riser <= 8.25`, never `> 8.25`: a dropped "not"); elsewhere (one side of an "or", an if's test: a trigger such as "more than 30 inches" written `drop <= 30 or guard`) the law's or its exact opposite, so the edge stays where the law puts it. A number read "none" (a conversion, a count, a permission to exceed) is no limit. A number the verification file flags counts only if its flag resolves it (gives the second reading's comparison, or says it is part of one length); otherwise the rule is refused, for a person. The drafter is shown each piece's second reading, comparisons and flags.
   A chain (`0 <= x < 36`) is an "and" of its comparisons; a number in arithmetic keeps its direction where it can be told (`riser > 8.25 + 0` is refused), and one read "none" is a term only in a formula of the law's kind (a measure times a number), never a limit hidden by `+ 0`; membership (`in`, `not in`) is equality; a range's lower number is an at-least and its upper an at-most; a count or degree never backs a length; a number the check can't place is reported.
   - *Known limits:* inside an "or", a reversed limit (`stair_spiral or riser > 8.25`) has the same edge as the right one and passes this check; an if's test written the wrong way round (`27 if stair_handrail_sides >= 1 else 31.5`, which gives one-sided stairs the two-sided width) passes it too, its number being checked by value only; so does a number whose direction arithmetic hides (a divisor), and the direction of a law-style formula (`tread <= 20 - 4/3 * riser`), whose numbers are read "none". The worked examples and the answer key comparison are what catch those.
4. **Part kinds added** (`parts.yaml`, engineer-written): basement, habitable attic and loft room, the room a sleeping loft is open to (R319.1 needs an escape opening in each, as in a sleeping room; an escape opening may sit within any of the four; a sleeping loft is not a sleeping room: it is part of the story below, R315.1, and its escape opening is in its room), handrail (within a stair), open side (of any walking surface: guards), sleeping loft (a space the architect marks as one that R315 applies to); and measures on escape openings (operable, opens to a yard, door type, under a deck and its path, below grade and its area well, into a patio cover), stairs (width at the handrail, handrail sides, risers, variations, spiral, serves a loft) and exit doors (side-hinged). A stair's clear width is now its width above the handrail (R318.7.1 sets two widths).
5. **Only architect-marked escape openings are checked; a sleeping room with none marked fails** (Ben, 2026-11-12; for the architects to confirm). So R319.1's rule is "for each escape opening within each sleeping room (basement, habitable attic, loft room), when none: fail".
6. **The answer key is independent.** A helper that never saw a draft wrote `test_data/answer_keys/new_york/new_york_state/general.yaml` from the law, the labels, the second reading and the parts list, before the drafting run (the commits show the order); the drafter never reads `test_data/`. `rule_drafting/book_eval.py` compares them rule by rule: the rules of one piece for one part kind on each side, every worked example of either side run through both sides' rows, part by part. Every disagreement is resolved by reading the law in `rule_drafting/evals/2026-11-13_house_egress/resolutions.yaml`; a test fails while one isn't. A key that is wrong is fixed with the reason. A draft that is wrong is fixed, never left in the rulebook: re-drafted with corrected instructions (a recorded call), or changed after the draft with the change recorded in the rule (`revised`: one entry per field, who, when, why with the law, as a label's revisions are; a rule added whole is `field: rule` and isn't credited to the drafting script). The AI's own rules stay in `drafted/records/as_drafted/` (written by every drafting run beside the drafts), and a test holds that a draft differs from them only in the fields its `revised` names, and that the copy's formulas are the recorded calls' answers. A re-draft replaces a revised rule; the drafting log names it (`revisions_replaced`) so a person re-applies the change, and the comparison test fails until then.
7. **What the drafter is told beyond zoning's instructions** (`book.BOOK_NOTE`), each added after a first run's drafts were compared with the key: tie a rule to the part it sits in (`within`) only where the law does ("every sleeping room shall have"), otherwise check every part of the kind (the first run checked escape-opening size only in sleeping rooms, missing basements and attics); a doubt about one kind of part is a flag on each such part, never on the whole building; never `==` (a measured length never equals a formula; also refused by `code_numbers`); never write that architects confirm (approval is deferred); a part an exception sends to a section not yet drafted (a spiral stair, R318.7.11.1) needs review, never not applicable; a sleeping loft's escape opening is in its room. The related sections shown with a piece are the rule and flag pieces that name it or a section above it (R315.5.2 lets a loft's stair follow other limits than R318.7's), Appendix BO left out.
8. **The first comparison** (before the fixes in 9): 84 disagreements from 19 causes, every one resolved by the law in `resolutions.yaml`, and a test checks each verdict against its rows (drafts_stricter: the drafts further from a pass on every row; key_stricter the key; a wrong draft without a false-pass risk, and a difference both sides' readings of the law allow, never pass where the key fails). The key was fixed seven times, each recorded in `key_fixes` and in the rule's notes: R315.5.2.2 tested the tallest riser against the 7 in minimum (the drafts had it right); exception 4's path is now in `opening_opens_to_yard`'s meaning, as the key assumed; R318.2's two-unit check moved to a review_when when the number check was tightened; spiral stairs need review until R318.7.11.1 is drafted (they were not applicable, unchecked); the room a sleeping loft is open to is a part (`loft_room`) with its escape opening checked; a loft stair that mixes R315.5.2's and R318.7's limits (risers from one, width from the other) needs review, not a pass (open for the architects). Of the 7 causes where the drafts are wrong, 2 could pass what the law fails: counting egress doors against dwelling units (two doors serving one unit pass), and checking the room a sleeping loft is open to only when it is labeled (a marked loft whose room isn't labeled is never checked). The other 5 never pass where the key fails or needs review (they give needs review where the law fails, or answer where the key says not applicable). The rest are stricter than the law, or come from how an example is shaped. The drafts stay draft. (All drafting of this book so far, two full runs and re-drafts after each change to the instructions: 109 recorded calls, $3.32; `python -m rule_drafting cost --state new_york/new_york_state`.)
9. **Every wrong draft fixed after the comparison** (review round 5, 2026-11-13, Claude for Ben; `resolutions.yaml` `draft_fixes`, each change in the rule's `revised`; no AI spend). The two false passes: R318.2's door count became a check of each marked egress door (none marked: fail; more than one dwelling unit: needs review, which door serves which unit isn't in the model), and a flag on each sleeping loft was added so a person checks its room was labeled. Fixing the full-opening flag (now a rule on `opening_fully_openable`) showed a third: a 36 in area well with a ladder passed though the ladder's reach into it isn't measured; a well with a ladder now fails under 30 in and needs review from 30 in up. Also fixed: a sleeping room with none marked fails; the ladder rule is for below-grade openings only; a handrail too high along its whole length fails. Three stricter-than-the-law causes that failed a lawful house were fixed the same way (a loft stair 17 to 20 in at the handrail needs review; loft stairs are left out of R318.7.1's widths and a loft stair within R318.7.5's risers needs review under R315.5.2.2; R321.1.2 and R321.1.3 apply only to required guards, a drop over 30 in, except that a guard top serving as the stair's handrail is held to R320.2's 34 to 38 in at any drop, with R320.2 exception 2's review for a top above 38 in only at a fitting, in the drafts and, as a key fix, in the key: review rounds 6 and 7). The stricter causes left only add needs review, never a fail (a test holds it), and are kept as known differences with the law cited. A test runs every case a fix settled on the fixed drafts (they agree with the key) and on the AI's own rules (they don't). After the fixes: 45 disagreements from 10 causes, none a wrong draft.

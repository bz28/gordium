# House egress rules: drafted by AI from New York's code, checked by code, graded against a hand key (2026-11-13)

**Purpose:** how Gordium's first building-code rules (egress for one- and two-family houses, 2025 Residential Code of New York State) were made, and why each check is there.\
**For:** engineers changing `rule_format/code_numbers.py`, `rule_format/parts.yaml`, `rule_drafting/book.py` or `book_eval.py`; Ben; the architects reading the rules.\
**Kept current by:** the engineer who changes those files, in that PR.

Egress step 3 of the [egress design doc](https://claude.ai/artifact/SzJvw7FdbsdfGMqUHxspFU) (§9, "Write the rules"). Step 1 brought in the law ([2026-11-12_building_code_text.md](2026-11-12_building_code_text.md)); step 2 made rules repeat per part ([2026-11-12_rules_for_each_part.md](2026-11-12_rules_for_each_part.md)).

## Decisions

1. **Same method as zoning.** One recorded AI call per piece of the law, the answer forced into the rule format, every draft checked by code (exact quotes, valid formulas, worked examples with pass, fail and "none", provenance), retried up to twice, then listed for a person. `python -m rule_drafting draft --state new_york/new_york_state --all --yes`. Drafts and every call's record go to `new_york_state/5_building_code_rules/drafted/` (a state folder's rulebooks are `5_building_code_rules/`, not `5_zoning_rules/`: its law is a building code). Drafts stay draft (approval deferred, 2026-10-19).
2. **What is drafted** (`book.SCOPE`, 25 pieces, all labeled rule): R318.2 exit door; R318.7.1, .2, .5.1, .5.2, .8 stairs (width, headroom, risers 8 1/4 in at most and treads 9 in at least, New York's own numbers, handrails); R319.1 to R319.4.2 escape openings (required, size, height and width, sill, under decks, doors, area wells and ladders); BF103.2 escape openings into a patio cover; R320.2 and R320.4 handrails; R321.1.1 to R321.1.3 guards; R315.2, R315.3, R315.5.2.1, R315.5.2.2 for a space marked a sleeping loft. **Appendix BO** (existing buildings) stays flags in the labels: whether a stair exists, was lawful when built, and the class of the work are the architect's input. Not drafted yet: ramps, landings, R318.7.3 and the special stairs, R320.3/.5/.6, R319.4.2.1/.2.2, R319.5 to R319.7.
3. **Numbers come from the second reading, never the extracted words alone** (`rule_format/code_numbers.py`, used by validation for every rule citing a book with a `text_verification.yaml`, and by the drafter's number binding). Each number a formula uses must be one of the cited piece's second-reading numbers that its quote states, in the measure's unit (8 1/4 inches is 8.25; a feet number followed by an inches number is one length: 6 feet 8 inches is 80 for a measure in inches). Its comparison must fit the second reading's: where the comparison must hold for the formula to hold (the whole check or applies_when, a part of a top "and", a "not" turned round by De Morgan) it must be the law's own (`riser <= 8.25`, never `> 8.25`: a dropped "not"); elsewhere (one side of an "or", an if's test: a trigger such as "more than 30 inches" written `drop <= 30 or guard`) the law's or its exact opposite, so the edge stays where the law puts it. A number read "none" (a conversion, a count, a permission to exceed) is no limit. A number the verification file flags counts only if its flag resolves it (gives the second reading's comparison, or says it is part of one length); otherwise the rule is refused, for a person. The drafter is shown each piece's second reading, comparisons and flags.
   - *Known limit:* inside an "or", a reversed limit (`stair_spiral or riser > 8.25`) has the same edge as the right one and passes this check; the worked examples and the answer key comparison are what catch it there.
4. **Part kinds added** (`parts.yaml`, engineer-written): basement and habitable attic (R319.1 needs an escape opening in each, as in a sleeping room; an escape opening may sit within any of the three), handrail (within a stair), open side (of any walking surface: guards), sleeping loft (a space the architect marks as one that R315 applies to); and measures on escape openings (operable, opens to a yard, door type, under a deck and its path, below grade and its area well, into a patio cover), stairs (width at the handrail, handrail sides, risers, variations, spiral, serves a loft) and exit doors (side-hinged). A stair's clear width is now its width above the handrail (R318.7.1 sets two widths).
5. **Only architect-marked escape openings are checked; a sleeping room with none marked fails** (Ben, 2026-11-12; for the architects to confirm). So R319.1's rule is "for each escape opening within each sleeping room (basement, habitable attic), when none: fail".
6. **The answer key is independent.** A helper that never saw a draft wrote `test_data/answer_keys/new_york/new_york_state/general.yaml` from the law, the labels, the second reading and the parts list, before the drafting run (the commits show the order); the drafter never reads `test_data/`. `rule_drafting/book_eval.py` compares them rule by rule: the rules of one piece for one part kind on each side, every worked example of either side run through both sides' rows, part by part. Every disagreement is resolved by reading the law in `rule_drafting/evals/2026-11-13_house_egress/resolutions.yaml`; a test fails while one isn't. Drafts are never edited by hand: a draft that is wrong is recorded as wrong (and re-drafted, or listed for a person); a key that is wrong is fixed with the reason.
7. **Budget:** the drafting cap is $20.00 (as Ben set it on the drafting branches #21 and #40); the ledger is shared by every checkout.

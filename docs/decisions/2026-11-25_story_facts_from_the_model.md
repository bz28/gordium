# The story facts, worked out from the model (2026-11-25)

**Purpose:** how Gordium works out the story facts the Building Code's single-exit tables read (which story is the first, second or fourth above grade plane; the dwelling units on a story; whether its rooms exit directly outside) from the model, instead of asking the architect.\
**For:** engineers changing `model_measurement/routes/story_place.py` (and the later story facts); reviewers of the rules that read them.\
**Kept current by:** the engineer who changes how a story fact is worked out, in that same branch.

Ben, 2026-11-25: everything the model can show is worked out by Gordium; only what it can't know for certain goes to the architect. Before this, the five facts had no source at all (no field fed them), so the rules reading them (BCNYS 1006.3.3, Tables 1006.3.4(1) and (2), 1007.1.1) always needed review on a real model.

## The law (2025 Building Code of New York State, quoted from the book)

- **GRADE PLANE** (p.48): "A reference plane representing the average of finished ground level adjoining the building at exterior walls. Where the finished ground level slopes away from the exterior walls, the reference plane shall be established by the lowest points within the area between the building and the lot line or, where the lot line is more than 6 feet (1829 mm) from the building, between the building and a point 6 feet (1829 mm) from the building."
- **STORY ABOVE GRADE PLANE** (p.63): "Any story having its finished floor surface entirely above grade plane, or in which the finished surface of the floor next above is: 1. More than 6 feet (1829 mm) above grade plane; 2. More than 12 feet (3658 mm) above the finished ground level at any point; or 3. More than 6 feet (1829 mm) above the finished ground level for more than 50 percent of the total building perimeter."
- **BASEMENT** (p.34): "A story that is not a story above grade plane".
- Table 1006.3.4(2) (p.317): "First story above or below grade plane", "Second story above grade plane"; Table 1006.3.4(1): "Basement, first, second or third story above grade plane", "Fourth story above grade plane and higher".

## Step 1: a story's place (this PR)

1. **The stories** are the levels with rooms, each with the range of its rooms' finished floor elevations and its level's own elevation (a sunken room widens it). A split level (two stories' floors overlapping in height) leaves every place not measured. Every level of the model must be listed (`all_levels`: the Revit reader sends every one since review round 2, one with nothing drawn on it too), else nothing is placed; a description not read whole (a room on no level read) places nothing either. A level with no rooms (not at a story's floor) may be a story nobody drew rooms on (a garage, a lobby, an unfinished basement) or no story (a roof, a foundation datum): both are tried (up to 8 such levels), so a datum under a slab changes nothing, but a garage level under the first rooms leaves their number open.
2. **The ground** is only what the architect stated, from the lot facts:
   - **flat ground** (`ground_is_flat`, `grade_elevation`): the grade plane at most that elevation, and a known point of finished ground (item 2). Finished ground stated higher at an opening contradicts it (nothing placed). Flat ground the host only *assumed* (`ground_assumed`) is not used.
   - **spot grades**, finished only (the Revit add-in's "Finished spot grades", or ground the architect states finished, Rhino's "No regrading near the house"; Revit's plain "Spot grades" are the existing ground, as Rhino's Grade layer, so they place nothing): every wall of the stories' outline, a courtyard's included, must have a spot within 2 ft outside that story's own outline (the zoning reader's own reach), else nothing is placed: the ground floor's walls under an upper story's overhang keep their spots. A spot inside a story's outline is under it, not ground beside it. Outlines that come apart (separate buildings) aren't placed. **Gordium's reading** (the usual surveying reading, said with every figure measured from spots and in the rules' notes): the ground between two spots lies between their values, so the average along the walls, and so the grade plane, is at most the highest spot. The slope-away rule can only lower the grade plane, with no lower end the spots show: a story is never proven a basement from spot grades. A spot is never item 2's "finished ground level at any point": it may stand in an areaway or a window well, whose floor as "ground" is open question 4.6 (docs/questions_for_architects.md); only flat ground gives that point.
   - **existing ground** (`grade_is: existing`), or **none**: nothing is placed.
3. **Each story** is certainly above grade plane (at the grade plane's top end, with 1/16 in to spare) or could be either: **a story is never proven a basement** (review round 6: that needs the finished ground at every point beside the building, and rounds 3 to 6 each found another way the model can hold ground below the stated ground or a higher floor next above). Below the top story, being above grade plane holds for every story above one that is (each test only gets easier higher up), so those readings are "the lowest b stories are basements"; the top story has no floor next above, so only its own floor decides it, whatever is under it (a lower story can be above grade plane by item 2 alone while the top one's floor is under the grade plane: then the top isn't placed). **A fact is measured only where every reading gives the same answer**, else it needs review and says which stories it could be. A slab at or above the stated ground is the first story above grade plane, and the stories over it are numbered; a building with a basement, or a level with no rooms under its lowest rooms (a foundation datum), leaves the numbers above open: the level mark "not a story" below settles a datum or roof level.
4. **Plumbing:** `check._story_ground` reads the lot facts into `story_place.ground_of`; `egress.check(..., ground=)` hands it to `routes.measure`.

**How we know:** `packages/model_measurement/tests/test_routes_story_place.py` walks 15 stacks of floors (basements, sunken rooms, floors on each threshold) with 5 sets of levels with no rooms (none, a garage under the rooms, a datum, a roof, a gap) over 21 grounds and none: an oracle written from the three definitions lists every world the stated ground allows (a grade plane anywhere at or below its top end, the lowest ground, the ground along half the perimeter, which height of a stepped floor counts as "the floor next above", each level with no rooms a story or not) and every fact measured must be the oracle's one value; flat ground must settle every stack whose lowest story is above it and clear of the thresholds. The real models: the four-unit apartment (as sent, its levels not all listed and no ground: not placed; with every level listed and its flat ground at 0, Level 1 first and Level 2 second; at 5 ft, Level 1 the first above or below and Level 2 not placed), and 106 Ithaca Rd's levels and four spot grades (Level 1 the first story; the Roof tried as a story and not). Through the check (`packages/design_checker/tests/test_story_place_check.py`), a one-exit Group A version of the apartment fails Table 1006.3.4(2) on flat ground at 0 (its second story), needs review at 5 ft, and needs review, never a pass, wherever the place isn't measured.

**Not done:** the Residential Code's stated `stories_above_grade_plane` isn't used as a cross-check (a different book: Ben through the coordinator, 2026-11-25). A level whose rooms' floor area is at most 2/3 of the story below's outline (or a room not read, or no outline) may be a mezzanine (505.2: part of the story below): tried both ways, never placed itself (review round 7).

**The level mark Gordium: Not a story** (follow-up PR): a level the architect ticks so (`all_levels[].not_a_story` true: a reference or datum level, a roof level) is never tried as a story nobody drew rooms on, nor taken as a level within a story's floors, nor as the lowest level for the undercroft check: the places come out exactly as if the level weren't in the model, and the levels set aside are said with every place (`levels_not_stories`, the architect's statement). A level so marked with rooms on it, or an enclosed space with no room drawn in it, disagrees with the mark: no story is placed. A value other than true or false places nothing; left out, null or false is not said (tried both ways, as before). **How we know:** `test_routes_story_place.py` walks every stack, set of levels with no rooms and ground with those levels marked: every place equals the one measured without them (the main walk checks that against the oracle), and reverting the filter fails 16 tests; the datum under a slab, unsettled before, now places the slab first and the next story second. **Review round 1 of the level mark (2026-11-26):** no false pass; two wording and display points fixed: the Revit tooltip now names an enclosed area with no Room as a disagreement too (with Gordium: No rooms on this level for a roof whose walls close one off), and a level ticked Not a story with Rooms drawn on it is listed first among the reader's level conflicts, as one ticked No rooms on this level is (`RouteTests.A_level_marked_not_a_story_with_rooms_on_it_is_a_conflict_said_first`). A cell of its own in the marking list's level row is left as a to-do: an unset cell there would mark every level row as needing marking. **Done (feat/marking-list-not-a-story):** the level row has the cell; blank, it says what it means (the level is tried both as a story and not) and never makes the row one to mark; a level is listed for it once it is set either way, its blank No rooms on this level then asking nothing where no enclosed area without a Room is on it (`RealModelTests.A_levels_not_a_story_cell_shows_its_blank_and_never_marks_a_level_to_mark`).

## Steps 2 and 3 (next PRs)

2. **Rooms that exit directly outside** (1006.3.4 item 2: "Rooms, areas and spaces complying with Section 1006.2.1 with exits that discharge directly to the exterior at the level of exit discharge, are permitted to have one exit or access to a single exit", p.317). Done (`routes.rooms_exit_outside`), read as **spaces** (the item's own "rooms, areas and spaces"; Gordium's reading, shown to confirm): rooms joined by doors or passages without crossing a corridor, a lobby, an exit or the outside are one space, whose occupants reach its exit through it (1016.2: a bedroom off a living room, a workroom or closet off a shop); a corridor or lobby opening into one space only is a vestibule of it, one opening into two or more is shared (`routes/spaces.py`). Yes only where every space has its own door to the outside marked as an exterior exit door at discharge. No only where a space reaches the outside through a corridor shared with another tenant, which the model shows only for dwelling units: every room of the hall is marked a corridor (a lobby may be a dwelling's own foyer), it reaches an exit itself, it is the space's one way out of itself (no other door or opening, into an exit or another hall, and no stair, ramp or ladder within it: the stairs read and placed outside it, and ramps, alternating tread devices and ship's ladders said to be none), two or more of the spaces opening into it hold a room whose function is residential, and the building states no corridor is within a dwelling unit (`corridors_within_dwelling_units` no, a fact the Building Code check already reads for 1020.2 exception 2). From the drawing alone a corridor within one tenant's space (a shop's back corridor to its toilets, a house's hall) can't be told from one shared between tenants. Not measured otherwise (a door outside not marked so, or onto an exterior stair; a space whose way out is through a corridor that may be its own, or neither; the story's routes not read). The rules read it only in their review conditions: yes keeps a one-exit story at needs review, no lets it fail where it fails, so it never makes a pass. **How we know:** `packages/model_measurement/tests/test_routes_rooms_exit_outside.py` walks a row of three rooms over every mix of door kinds (marked, not marked, onto a stair, none) and joins (door, passage, wall), and again with a corridor each room opens into or not (shared, a vestibule, or opening into none), with and without its own exit, the rooms residential or with no function, the corridor fact stated no or not stated: every mix, against an oracle written separately from the reading above; the four-unit apartment is no on both stories once its apartments' function and the corridor fact are stated (its apartments reach the outside only through the shared corridor and the stair enclosures, which are exits); as Revit sent it, not measured.
3. **Dwelling units on a story** (Table 1006.3.4(1): "MAXIMUM NUMBER OF DWELLING UNITS", 4 on a one-exit story). Done (`routes/dwelling_units.py`): the groups of rooms joined by doors or passages without crossing a corridor, a lobby, an exit or the outside (`routes/spaces.py`, the same grouping as step 2). A group with a room whose function is residential, every lived-in room stating a function, and **one** way out to those shared spaces (a door, or an opening with no door) is one unit; a group whose lived-in rooms all state a listed function a dwelling unit can't be made of is none (toilets, closets and storerooms aren't lived in; every Table 1004.5 function but residential, dormitory and institutional sleeping); a group with a lived-in room stating no function or one not on the list (it may be a room shared between units, or a typo), a stair, ramp or ladder within it or not ruled out (the upper part of a unit whose door is downstairs), a closet, toilet or storeroom, or a room not itself residential, opening onto two or more of its rooms (shared by two units), two or more such doors (two units joined by a door, a lock-off), or none (part of a unit on two stories), a room with no use given (it may be a corridor joining two units), or a story whose routes aren't read: not measured. **Gordium's reading, shown to the architect to confirm** (with every row reading the count: the new part key `rests_on`, said beside the row, never holding a pass): a group with a residential room and one way out to the shared spaces is one dwelling unit, which holds only if every room shared between dwelling units is marked a corridor or lobby (a shared vestibule or landing marked residential would join two units into one) and BCNYS 1016.2.1 (p.341) is met: "each tenant space, dwelling unit and sleeping unit shall be provided with access to the required exits without passing through adjacent tenant spaces, dwelling units and sleeping units". A second unit drawn with no door of its own would break 1016.2.1 itself, a flag (a person's check) in every answer, Group R-2 included (Ben through the coordinator, 2026-11-25: keep the pass, make the dependence visible). A hallway inside a unit marked a corridor makes each room off it a "unit": a count too high, which only ever fails the row; a whole unit marked a corridor, lobby or exit would instead drop out of the count, so an exit stating any function, or a corridor or lobby stating any function but a listed other use and opening into no room of a dwelling unit, stops the count (review rounds 8-11), and the words beside the count also name "no room of a dwelling unit is marked a corridor, lobby or exit". If the not-measured case turns out common on real models, a room mark (Gordium: Unit) follows. **How we know:** `packages/model_measurement/tests/test_routes_dwelling_units.py` walks three suites on a corridor (residential, business or no function; one or two rooms; no, one or two doors to the corridor; neighbours joined by a door or not) against an oracle from DWELLING UNIT (202) and 1016.2.1; the four-unit apartment has 2 units a story once its function is marked (as Revit sent it: not measured, no function); through the check (`packages/design_checker/tests/test_dwelling_units_check.py`) the one-exit R-2 apartment with an NFPA 13R system passes Table 1006.3.4(1) on its measured counts with the reading beside each row, and wherever a measured count lets that row pass the 1016.2.1 flag needs review in the same answer.

## The unit mark, Gordium: Unit (follow-up PR to step 3)

Step 3's count has two disclosed limits, said beside every count: a room shared between units but marked residential joins them into one, and a unit's room marked a corridor, lobby or exit that states no function drops out. Both are the drawing not saying which unit a room is in, so the architect says it: the room mark **Gordium: Unit** (`rooms[].unit`, the unit's name, the same on every room of it). Where any room of a story has one, the names replace the grouping (`dwelling_units._marked`): the count is the number of names, said beside the row as the architect's statement (`architect_stated`), and the grouping's reading and its 1016.2.1 dependence aren't said (the count no longer stands on them; the 1016.2.1 flag is in every Group R-2 answer anyway). The marks need no function, no single way out and no stair check: a two-story unit's own stair, a balcony door, a bath between two of its rooms no longer stop the count. With no mark on a story, step 3 runs as before.

The two limits close this way: a room shared between units with no mark, stating any function a dwelling unit may have (or none), stops the count with the fix said (mark its unit, or its use Corridor or Lobby); a unit's room marked a corridor or lobby counts by its mark. What is left is said beside the count: every room of a dwelling unit on the story carries its unit's name (a unit none of whose rooms is marked, all of them marked halls or exits, would still be left out where they state no function, or a residential one and open into rooms of two or more named units, as a shared lobby does: the drawing can't tell them apart). Not measured where the marks and the drawing disagree or don't settle it: a mark that isn't one name (not text or a whole number; two or more, "1A, 1B", "1A and 1B", "1A/1B"); two names differing only in capitals or in where spaces stand (spaces at the ends and repeated spaces are no new name); a unit whose rooms on the story aren't joined to each other through its own rooms (two units given one name?); a unit whose lived-in rooms all state a listed other use; a room the drawing takes as an exit given a unit; an unmarked exit stating a function, or an unmarked corridor or lobby stating a function a unit may have that opens into rooms of fewer than two named units (review rounds 1 and 2). A two-story unit counts on each story it has a room on (Table 1006.3.4(1) counts the units on a story; more is never a pass, as the row reads `story_dwelling_units <= 4`). A hidden door changes no count from the marks (it can only stop it, through the joined-rooms check), so step 3's `links_read` isn't needed here.

**How we know:** `packages/model_measurement/tests/test_routes_dwelling_units.py` walks unit marks (none, A, B) on each of four rooms over every function per suite and every join, against an oracle written apart (the names, or not measured); the shared vestibule marked residential counts 1 with no marks, stops with the units marked, and counts 2 once it is a lobby; a unit's room marked a corridor or lobby counts 3 of 3; marks naming two units, alike names, a mark not a name each stop; the four-unit apartment marked counts 2 a story with the marks' words.

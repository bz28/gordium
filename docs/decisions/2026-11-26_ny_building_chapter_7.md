# New York's Building Code Chapter 7, the parts that protect the means of egress: the plan (2026-11-26)

**Purpose:** which sections of Chapter 7 (fire and smoke protection features) of the 2025 Building Code of New York State Gordium brings in for egress, what is a rule and what a flag, where the rules go, and the order of the PRs.\
**For:** engineers extending `new_york_state_building/`; Ben (scope, order); reviewers of each part's PR.\
**Kept current by:** the engineer who drafts a part, in that same branch (the part's status line below).

Follows [Chapter 10's plan](2026-11-23_ny_building_egress_all_occupancies.md). Chapter 10 says what the means of egress are; for how its enclosures, corridor walls and doors are built it sends the reader to Chapter 7: an interior exit stairway's enclosure is built "as fire barriers in accordance with Section 707" (1023.2), its openings and doors "in accordance with Section 716" (1023.4), its penetrations per 714 (1023.5); a rated corridor's walls are "fire partitions" per 708 (1020.2); an exit access stairway's enclosure is a shaft enclosure per 713 (1019.3); areas of refuge are behind smoke barriers per 709 (1009.6.4). This plan brings those sections in, the same pipeline as Chapter 10's (sources.yaml, extraction, every number read a second time from the page images, labels, rules or flags, coverage).

## Decisions

1. **One rulebook file.** The checker reads one rulebook per law folder (`rule_format.rulebook.building_code_rulebook`: `5_building_code_rules/general.yaml` or `drafted/general.yaml`; `place.inherited_rulebooks`, `design_checker/egress.laws`, `permit_sheets/source.py` and the coverage report all read that one file). So Chapter 7's rules go in `drafted/general.yaml`, as their own block in book order: after 508's rules and before Chapter 10's first (1004.7), so the Chapter 10 work appending at the end, and Chapter 4's before 508, touch other lines of the file and merge without conflict. Pinned by `test_chapter_7_rules_sit_in_book_order_before_chapter_10`. A file per chapter would need all four readers changed together and every fingerprint re-recorded: not worth it while three chapters are drafted at once.
2. **What the model can't show is a flag.** Nothing these sections ask is in the model yet: an enclosure's fire-resistance rating, how many stories it connects (the model may hold only some floors), its walls' construction and continuity above a ceiling, penetrations, joints, ducts, a door's fire protection rating, closers and labels. Each piece that sets a requirement is a flag, one per piece, a person's check for every building (never narrowed to a fact the model could get wrong, never a pass). A rule comes only where a stated or measured fact settles it (below).
3. **Only the egress parts.** Sections that are no part of the means of egress are not brought in: materials by type of construction (707.2, 713.3), the fire barriers of atriums, incidental uses, control areas, separated occupancies, fire areas and horizontal separation offsets (707.3.6 to 707.3.11, Table 707.3.10), penthouse mechanical rooms (713.12.1) and chutes (713.13). sources.yaml names the table on the pages read.

## Rules that new facts would allow (the Revit-fields queue)

With a stated fact, three checks become rules, each never a pass where the fact is missing:
- **An exit enclosure's rating** (1023.2, 707.3.2, 713.4): 2 hours always passes (none asks more), under 1 hour fails unless 1023.2's exceptions (Group I-3's 408.3.8, an atrium's 404.6, 510.2's horizontal separation) aren't ruled out; between them it needs the stories the enclosure connects. Fact: the enclosure room's stated rating in hours (a room mark like the corridor's "Gordium: Corridor rating minutes", PR #229), and the stories it connects (from the levels where the model holds every floor, else stated).
- **A door's fire protection rating** in an exit enclosure or exit passageway (Table 716.1(2): 1 1/2 hours in a 2-hour enclosure, 1 hour in a 1-hour one) and in a rated corridor (1/3 hour): fact, the door's stated rating (Revit's door type Fire Rating parameter where it reads as a duration, else a mark). A 510.2 podium's 3-hour enclosure below the separation asks a 3-hour door, so "1 1/2 hours passes" holds only where 510.2 is ruled out.
- **A door's closer and latch** (713.7, 716.2.6.1): fact, the door's stated closing (self-closing, automatic on smoke detection, none).

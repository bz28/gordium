# Which New York code a building's egress is checked against (2026-11-22)

**Purpose:** how Check project decides, for each building, whether New York's 2025 Residential Code (RCNYS) or its 2025 Building Code (BCNYS) governs its egress, from the law, and what Gordium does when it can't tell.\
**For:** engineers wiring a check or a host to it; Ben (the open points at the end); architects (why a check says "needs review: which code").\
**Kept current by:** the engineer who changes `packages/design_checker/design_checker/code_scope.py`, in that PR.

Follows [the BCNYS decision](2026-11-21_ny_building_code.md) ("What is next: wire it into Check project"). Quotes from Public.Resource.Org's copies (the RCNYS, sha256 36490d8c..., and the BCNYS, 729d2db7..., the same files the state folders' `sources.yaml` name), PDF pages; `test_code_scope.py` reads every quote again from its page.

## The law

- **RCNYS R101.2** (p.24): "The provisions of this code shall apply to ... the following buildings and structures, provided that such building or structure is not more than three stories above grade plane in height with a separate means of egress and their accessory structures not more than three stories above grade plane in height: 1. Detached one-family dwellings. 2. Detached two-family dwellings in which each dwelling unit has a separate means of egress. 3. Townhouses. 4. Bed and breakfast dwellings. 5. Live/work units located in townhouses and complying with the requirements of Section 508.5 of the Building Code of New York State. 6. Owner-occupied lodging houses that have five or fewer guestrooms and are provided with an automatic sprinkler system complying with Section P2904 of this code. 7. Manufactured homes used as dwelling units."
- **Its exception** (p.24): "Application of the provisions of the Building Code of New York State to ... the buildings and structures listed in Section R101.2 is permitted, provided that all applicable provisions of the Building Code of New York State are applied." The architect's one real choice (below).
- **BCNYS 101.2** (p.22): the BCNYS applies to "every building or structure"; exception 1 sends the same seven kinds (1.1 to 1.7), under the same three-story limit, to the RCNYS. The two books agree; no building falls between them.
- **What "three stories above grade plane" counts** (RCNYS R202 STORY ABOVE GRADE PLANE, p.52): "Any story having its finished floor surface entirely above grade plane, or in which the finished surface of the floor next above is either of the following: 1. More than 6 feet ... above grade plane. 2. More than 12 feet ... above the finished ground level at any point. 3. More than 6 feet ... above the finished ground level for more than 50 percent of the total building perimeter." A habitable attic is one (R316.3, p.98: "A habitable attic shall be considered a story above grade plane", with exceptions for small ones).
- **Dwellings and townhouses** (R202): DWELLING "Any building that contains one or two dwelling units" (p.40); TOWNHOUSE "A building that contains three or more attached townhouse units"; TOWNHOUSE UNIT "A single-family dwelling unit in a townhouse that extends from foundation to roof, that has a yard or public way on not less than two sides, and has a separate means of egress" (p.53).
- **Care and other uses in a dwelling** (BCNYS): five or fewer persons receiving custodial care "shall be classified as Group R-3 or shall comply with the Residential Code ... provided an automatic sprinkler system is installed" (308.2.4, p.78; likewise 308.3.2, 305.2.3, 308.5.4 and 310.4.1, p.80); more is a Building Code occupancy (E, I, R-4). A LIVE/WORK UNIT is "a dwelling unit or sleeping unit in which a significant portion of the space includes a nonresidential use that is operated by the tenant" (202, p.52): under the RCNYS only in a townhouse (R101.2 item 5). Group R-2 is "more than two dwelling units where the occupants are primarily permanent" (310.3, p.80).

Searched beyond the two books' text: DOS's notice of adoption (https://dos.ny.gov/notice-adoption, read 2026-11-21, already cited by the BCNYS decision). No New York interpretation of R101.2 was needed: every condition is in its own words. No architect question is open on the scope itself.

## Decisions

1. **One decider, plain code** (`code_scope.decide(values, building)`): the check's values (Ithaca's `use`, `stories`, `dwelling_units`, `attached_units_in_building`, `home_occupation`, `rooms_let_for_profit`, `accessory_dwelling_unit`, `adu_detached`) and the interior description's new `building` block (the architect's statements below). It returns the code, the book's title, why in plain words, the sections with their quotes, and, when it can't tell, what to state. Never the RCNYS by default.
2. **The order:** R101.2's exception (Follows the Building Code: Yes) → the BCNYS. No use → needs review. Occupancy groups stated other than R-3 → the BCNYS (or needs review if the use is a house word: a contradiction). A use word that is none of R101.2's dwellings (multiple family, school, day care...) → the BCNYS. Bed and breakfast inn → needs review (only an R202 bed and breakfast dwelling is the RCNYS's). A house word with care, a home occupation or rooms let → needs review (each falls to the BCNYS only on facts Gordium doesn't read). Attached one-family: under three units it is a two-family building; three or more are a townhouse only with Townhouse units: Yes. Two units (two-family, semi-detached pair, a house with an attached ADU) need Separate means of egress: Yes (No → the BCNYS, Group R-3). Then the stories: more than three → the BCNYS, three or fewer → the RCNYS.
3. **The stories count:** the architect's Stories above grade plane where stated. Else Ithaca's own count (`stories`) stands in, only as an upper bound: Ithaca's measuring gives a count only when nothing drawn is below the ground (`places/ithaca_city/measure.py` `_stories_whole`: a part below the ground is the basement question, and the count needs review), so there is no basement or cellar the State could count (R202 item 3 is the State's alone) and Ithaca doesn't; and what Ithaca leaves out the State does too (a mezzanine, R202 MEZZANINE; an attic "unfinished and without human occupancy": R202 ATTIC is inside the top STORY, and only a habitable attic is a story above grade plane, R316.3, which Ithaca counts). So Ithaca's three or fewer means the State's three or fewer. Ithaca's four or more doesn't settle it (a small habitable attic, R316.3's exceptions), so it needs review.
4. **Where it can't tell, both books run**, each section headed by a needs-review line ("Which code governs this building: can't tell yet", why, and what to state); a section never reads as waiting calmly or complying then. Where it chose the BCNYS, its section has one line saying why (counted toward nothing). **Where it chose the RCNYS, a house's check is exactly as before**: the same rows, results, items and sections (pinned: `test_code_scope.py`), the decision in the answer only.
5. **The answer carries it** (`which_code`, Ben 2026-11-22: chosen automatically, shown, never a dropdown): `{"code": "residential_code" | "building_code" | null, "title", "why", "cites": [{"section", "document", "page", "quote"}], "ask"}`, the decider's own words, for the panel to show at the top of the results. Null where the place's laws don't split buildings between books (a Maine place).
6. **The architect's statements are marks on Project Information**, read into the interior's `building` block only when set (`BuildingFacts`, `ProjectCheck.cs`): Gordium: Follows the Building Code (R101.2's exception, quoted in its tooltip), Stories above grade plane, Separate means of egress, Townhouse units, Care facility, Occupancy groups (BCNYS Chapter 3, comma separated), Sprinkler system (None, NFPA 13, NFPA 13R, NFPA 13D). A word or count Gordium can't read is asked again, nothing sent. The last two feed the BCNYS rulebook's proposed measures (`occupancy_group_i4`, `other_occupancy_present`, `sprinklered_903_3_1_1`, `sprinklered_903_3_1_2`).
7. **Ithaca inherits both folders**; each `state.yaml` says which buildings its law covers (`scope: residential_code | building_code`) and the BCNYS its own waiting words. The BCNYS's rules run on the route measuring's parts (`model_measurement.routes`: story, space, corridor); its whole-building flags each show as their own needs-review line (not applicable where their condition is stated false). A rule reading a figure not measured yet (occupant loads: no mark yet) needs review and says which.

## Worked example

The eval building (`test_data/test_sites/new_york/ithaca_city/four_unit_apartment/`): four apartments on two stories, Use multiple_family, Occupancy groups R-2, no sprinklers. `decide`: its use is none of R101.2's dwellings → `building_code`, "a building of Group R-2, none of the dwellings R101.2 lists (101.2)". Only the BCNYS runs. Each story's two exits are its stair enclosures' doors, 41.0 ft apart at their nearest points (x 9.5 to 50.5); the story's diagonal over its outside faces is sqrt(61² + 31²) = 68.425 ft; 41.0 / 68.425 = 0.599, at least one-half (1007.1.1, no sprinkler system): complies on both stories. The occupant-load rules need review (not read yet); the 23 flags are each a person's check; the Group I-4 rules are not applicable. Against it, 106 Ithaca Rd (one_family_detached, Stories 3, flat ground): `residential_code`, "a detached one-family dwelling, 3 stories above grade plane at most" (R101.2 item 1), and its check is what it was.

The Building Code's egress (#151) moves from "drafted, not run" to "runs in Check project for buildings it governs".

## Open points

- **Ben:** whether a care facility or home occupation should get its own statement (how many persons receive care; whether the unit is a live/work unit) so those houses can be decided instead of needing review. Today: needs review, with a by-hand check.
- **Not decided here:** a semi-detached pair is read as one two-family building (R202 DWELLING counts a building's units, not lots). This is the law's wording; no New York interpretation was found that reads a lot line as splitting a building.
- **Accessory structures** (R101.2: under the RCNYS when not more than three stories above grade plane): Check project decides for the main building; an accessory building's own code isn't decided yet.

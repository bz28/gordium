# Which New York code a building's egress is checked against (2026-11-22)

**Purpose:** how Check project decides, for each building, whether New York's 2025 Residential Code (RCNYS) or its 2025 Building Code (BCNYS) governs its egress, from the law, and what Gordium does when it can't tell.\
**For:** engineers wiring a check or a host to it; Ben (the open points at the end); architects (why a check says "needs review: which code").\
**Kept current by:** the engineer who changes `packages/design_checker/design_checker/code_scope.py`, in that PR.

Follows [the BCNYS decision](2026-11-21_ny_building_code.md) ("What is next: wire it into Check project"). Quotes from Public.Resource.Org's copies (the RCNYS, sha256 36490d8c..., and the BCNYS, 729d2db7..., the same files the state folders' `sources.yaml` name), PDF pages; `test_code_scope.py` reads every quote again from its page.

## The law

- **RCNYS R101.2** (p.24): "The provisions of this code shall apply to ... the following buildings and structures, provided that such building or structure is not more than three stories above grade plane in height with a separate means of egress and their accessory structures not more than three stories above grade plane in height: 1. Detached one-family dwellings. 2. Detached two-family dwellings in which each dwelling unit has a separate means of egress. 3. Townhouses. 4. Bed and breakfast dwellings. 5. Live/work units located in townhouses and complying with the requirements of Section 508.5 of the Building Code of New York State. 6. Owner-occupied lodging houses that have five or fewer guestrooms and are provided with an automatic sprinkler system complying with Section P2904 of this code. 7. Manufactured homes used as dwelling units."
- **Its exception** (p.24): "Application of the provisions of the Building Code of New York State to ... the buildings and structures listed in Section R101.2 is permitted, provided that all applicable provisions of the Building Code of New York State are applied." The architect's one real choice (below).
- **BCNYS 101.2** (p.22): the BCNYS applies to "every building or structure"; exception 1 sends the same seven kinds (1.1 to 1.7), under the same three-story limit, to the RCNYS. The two books agree; no building falls between them.
- **What "three stories above grade plane" counts** (RCNYS R202 STORY ABOVE GRADE PLANE, p.52): "Any story having its finished floor surface entirely above grade plane, or in which the finished surface of the floor next above is either of the following: 1. More than 6 feet ... above grade plane. 2. More than 12 feet ... above the finished ground level at any point. 3. More than 6 feet ... above the finished ground level for more than 50 percent of the total building perimeter." A habitable attic is one (R316.3, p.98: "A habitable attic shall be considered a story above grade plane", with exceptions for small ones).
- **Dwellings and townhouses** (R202): DWELLING "Any building that contains one or two dwelling units" (p.40); TOWNHOUSE "A building that contains three or more attached townhouse units"; TOWNHOUSE UNIT "A single-family dwelling unit in a townhouse that extends from foundation to roof, that has a yard or public way on not less than two sides, and has a separate means of egress" (p.53).
- **Care and other uses in a dwelling** (BCNYS): five or fewer persons receiving custodial care "shall be classified as Group R-3 or shall comply with the Residential Code ... provided an automatic sprinkler system is installed" (308.2.4, p.78; likewise 308.3.2, 305.2.3, 308.5.4 and 310.4.1, p.80); more is a Building Code occupancy (E, I, R-4). A LIVE/WORK UNIT is "a dwelling unit or sleeping unit in which a significant portion of the space includes a nonresidential use that is operated by the tenant" (202, p.52): under the RCNYS only in a townhouse (R101.2 item 5). Group R-2 is "more than two dwelling units where the occupants are primarily permanent" (310.3, p.80).

Searched beyond the two books' text: DOS's notice of adoption (https://dos.ny.gov/notice-adoption, read 2026-11-21, already cited by the BCNYS decision). No New York interpretation of R101.2 was needed: every condition is in its own words. No architect question is open on the scope itself.

## Decisions

1. **One decider, plain code** (`code_scope.decide(values, building)`): the check's values (Ithaca's `use`, `stories`, `dwelling_units`, `attached_units_in_building`, `home_occupation`, `rooms_let_for_profit`, `accessory_dwelling_unit`, `adu_detached`) and the interior description's new `building` block (the architect's statements below). It returns the code, the book's title, why in plain words, the sections with their quotes, and, when it can't tell, what to state. Never the RCNYS by default.
2. **The order:** R101.2's exception (Follows the Building Code: Yes) → the BCNYS. No use → needs review. Occupancy groups stated other than R-3 → the BCNYS (or needs review if the use is a house word: a contradiction). A use word that is none of R101.2's dwellings (multiple family, school, day care...) → the BCNYS. Bed and breakfast inn → needs review (only an R202 bed and breakfast dwelling is the RCNYS's). A house word with care, a home occupation or rooms let stated → needs review (each falls to the BCNYS only on facts Gordium doesn't read). **The building's dwelling units are counted for every use word** (review round 1): the use's own (one; two for a two-family or a semi-detached pair; the whole attached building's for an attached one-family), one more for an accessory dwelling unit in or on the building (an ADU on the lot not said to be detached or not → needs review); the lot's stated dwelling units must agree with the use (else needs review). One unit → R101.2 item 1; two → item 2, which needs Separate means of egress: Yes (No → the BCNYS, Group R-3, 310.4); three or more in one building → the BCNYS (more than R202 DWELLING's "one or two"; Group R-2, 310.3), unless three or more attached units are all townhouse units (item 3, Townhouse units: Yes); a townhouse unit holding an ADU → needs review (R202: "a single-family dwelling unit"). Then the stories: more than three → the BCNYS, three or fewer → the RCNYS.
2a. **What isn't stated about a house is Gordium's reading, said out loud** (review round 1; Ben: best practice): a care facility, a home occupation, rooms let for profit or an ADU not stated is read as none, so the dwelling decides, and the answer says so: `which_code.assumes` lists each one assumed and `why` ends "Gordium assumes ... (not stated): if any is so, say it (Gordium: Care facility, Gordium: Home occupation, ...)". Why a reading and not needs review: each is a use of the house beyond the dwelling the architect named (Ithaca lists day care, home occupations, rooming and ADUs as uses or accessory uses of their own, §325-3B), so a stated one-family use without them is the ordinary case, and the law only moves a house to the BCNYS on a fact (BCNYS 308.2.4 and 310.4.1: more than five persons receiving care; 202 LIVE/WORK UNIT: a significant nonresidential portion; R101.2 item 6: more than five guestrooms or no sprinkler system; R202 DWELLING: a third unit). Stated, each is decided by the law as above. An ADU's kind stated (ADU detached Yes or No) with the ADU itself blank, or said to be none, needs review asking for Gordium: Accessory dwelling unit: never read as "no ADU" (review round 2). A fact the measuring set aside because it contradicts another (the occupant counts against the dwelling units, `measure.py fact_conflicts`) is never read as unstated: the decision needs review naming the conflict. Ithaca's dwelling units leave an ADU out (its own fact, as the occupant counts read it, and as the Gordium: Dwelling units tooltip says): only the use's own count agrees with the use; one more beside a stated ADU may or may not count the ADU in, so it needs review asking to correct Dwelling units (review round 3: never settled toward the Residential Code).
3. **The stories count:** the architect's Stories above grade plane where stated. Else Ithaca's own count (`stories`) stands in, only as an upper bound and **only where it was read from the drawing** (review round 1: `check.py` passes `stories_from_drawing`, true when the stories record names the drawn parts it counted and no measurement hit a geometry error; a count typed while nothing countable is drawn is kept by the measuring as stated, never checked against the ground, and Ithaca's count misses R202's item 3, a walk-out basement more than 6 ft above the ground for over half the perimeter, so it needs review asking for Stories above grade plane). Read from the drawing, Ithaca's measuring gives a count only when nothing drawn is below the ground (`places/ithaca_city/measure.py` `_stories_whole`: a part below the ground is the basement question, and the count needs review), so there is no basement or cellar the State could count (R202 item 3 is the State's alone) and Ithaca doesn't; and what Ithaca leaves out the State does too (a mezzanine, R202 MEZZANINE; an attic "unfinished and without human occupancy": R202 ATTIC is inside the top STORY, and only a habitable attic is a story above grade plane, R316.3, which Ithaca counts). So Ithaca's three or fewer means the State's three or fewer. **A typed count checked against the drawing is a safe bound too** (review round 2): `_stories_whole` keeps the architect's Gordium: Stories (with the parts it counted) only after it has checked that nothing drawn starts below the ground or floats above it, that no parts are apart or sunk into each other in plan, and that the count lies within what the drawing allows (never fewer than the floors drawn, never more than 7 ft a story could hold). With nothing drawn below the ground there is no basement or cellar R202's item 3 could count that Ithaca misses, so the architect's Ithaca count bounds the State's; Gordium takes the architect's stated facts at face value as everywhere. The answer's words name which it was: a count read from the drawn floors, or the stated Ithaca count compared with the drawing, nothing drawn below the ground (`code_scope.stories_bound`); which it was comes from the measuring's own flag on the stories record (`stated`, set by `_stories_whole` and kept when `_stories_over` merges candidate buildings), never from its words. Ithaca's four or more doesn't settle it (a small habitable attic, R316.3's exceptions), so it needs review.
4. **Where it can't tell, both books run**, each section headed by a needs-review line ("Which code governs this building: can't tell yet", why, and what to state); a section never reads as waiting calmly or complying then. Where it chose the BCNYS, its section has one line saying why (counted toward nothing). **Where it chose the RCNYS, a house's check is exactly as before**: the same rows, results, items and sections (pinned: `test_code_scope.py`), the decision in the answer only.
5. **The answer carries it** (`which_code`, Ben 2026-11-22: chosen automatically, shown, never a dropdown): `{"code": "residential_code" | "building_code" | null, "title", "why", "cites": [{"section", "document", "page", "quote"}], "ask", "assumes": [words]}`, the decider's own words, for the panel to show at the top of the results. Null where the place's laws don't split buildings between books (a Maine place).
6. **The architect's statements are marks on Project Information**, read into the interior's `building` block only when set (`BuildingFacts`, `ProjectCheck.cs`): Gordium: Follows the Building Code (R101.2's exception, quoted in its tooltip), Stories above grade plane, Separate means of egress, Townhouse units, Care facility, Occupancy groups (BCNYS Chapter 3, comma separated), Sprinkler system (None, NFPA 13, NFPA 13R, NFPA 13D). A word or count Gordium can't read is asked again, nothing sent. A building block that isn't a mapping, or a key that is no fact, is said (needs review), never dropped. The dwelling's own facts an ask names are marks too (review round 1), sent as the lot facts of the same names: Dwelling units, Units in the attached building, Home occupation, Rooms let for profit, Accessory dwelling unit, ADU detached. The last two feed the BCNYS rulebook's proposed measures (`occupancy_group_i4`, `other_occupancy_present`, `sprinklered_903_3_1_1`, `sprinklered_903_3_1_2`).
7. **Ithaca inherits both folders**; each `state.yaml` says which buildings its law covers (`scope: residential_code | building_code`) and the BCNYS its own waiting words. The BCNYS's rules run on the route measuring's parts (`model_measurement.routes`: story, space, corridor); its whole-building flags each show as their own needs-review line (not applicable where their condition is stated false). A rule reading a figure not measured yet (occupant loads: no mark yet) needs review and says which.

## Worked example

The eval building (`test_data/test_sites/new_york/ithaca_city/four_unit_apartment/`): four apartments on two stories, Use multiple_family, Occupancy groups R-2, no sprinklers. `decide`: its use is none of R101.2's dwellings → `building_code`, "a building of Group R-2, none of the dwellings R101.2 lists (101.2)". Only the BCNYS runs. Each story's two exits are its stair enclosures' doors, 41.0 ft apart at their nearest points (x 9.5 to 50.5); the story's diagonal over its outside faces is sqrt(61² + 31²) = 68.425 ft; 41.0 / 68.425 = 0.599, at least one-half (1007.1.1, no sprinkler system): complies on both stories. The occupant-load rules need review (not read yet); the 23 flags are each a person's check; the Group I-4 rules are not applicable. Against it, 106 Ithaca Rd (one_family_detached, Stories 3, flat ground): `residential_code`, "a detached one-family dwelling, 3 stories above grade plane at most" (R101.2 item 1), and its check is what it was.

## What changes in the System Map

The egress stage's flow gains a first step: "which code governs" (the decider, before any egress rule runs), with three outcomes (Residential Code, Building Code, can't tell: both, needs review). The key decisions list gains: "Which NY code: decided from the law (R101.2), shown, never chosen by the architect except R101.2's exception; can't tell is needs review." The Building Code's egress (#151) moves from "drafted, not run" to "runs in Check project for buildings it governs".

## Open points

- **Ben:** whether a care facility or home occupation should get its own statement (how many persons receive care; whether the unit is a live/work unit) so those houses can be decided instead of needing review. Today: needs review, with a by-hand check.
- **Not decided here:** a semi-detached pair is read as one two-family building (R202 DWELLING counts a building's units, not lots). This is the law's wording; no New York interpretation was found that reads a lot line as splitting a building.
- **Accessory structures** (R101.2: under the RCNYS when not more than three stories above grade plane): Check project decides for the main building; an accessory building's own code isn't decided yet.

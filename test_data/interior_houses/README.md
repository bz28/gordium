# Made-up houses with interiors

Six synthetic houses (none is a real house) written in the host-neutral [interior description](../../docs/interior/interior_description_format.md), so egress measuring can be tested end to end on any machine, without Rhino or Revit. Each folder has:

- `house.yaml`: the house's rooms, escape openings, stairs, guards and exit door, as simple geometry;
- `expected.yaml`: every measure worked out **by hand, with its working in a comment, before the measuring code ran on the house** (committed first, in its own commit). A measure the description can't settle is listed under `not_measured`; a list not given at all under `not_read`.

`packages/model_measurement/tests/test_interior_houses.py` checks the code's numbers against `expected.yaml` exactly (to a millionth), then runs the measured parts through the checker's per-part rows with SYNTHETIC rules (made-up rules, never the code's text).

**Drawn in a host.** A host that reads a house's inside itself (the Revit add-in) draws houses from their own data (`python -m model_measurement.interior.recordings draw`), reads them with its reader and records what it sent in `recorded/<host>/<house>.json`. `packages/model_measurement/tests/test_interior_recordings.py` measures every recording on every push and compares it with `expected.yaml` (`model_measurement/interior/recordings.py`): each building step of the [Revit interior plan](../../docs/revit/revit_interior_reading_design.md) (section 7) names its houses and the measures in its scope, which must equal the hand-worked ones; every other measure must come back not measured, never a value. `expected.yaml` is never edited to fit a host. Step 3 (rooms and escape windows): houses 1, 2 and 5; recorded in Revit 2027.4 in the Windows trial (`recorded/revit/`, `apps/revit_addin/README.md`). Step 4 (stairs): houses 2 and 3, each house recorded at the latest step that draws it. A host draws house 3's tall riser where it can: Revit makes a component run's risers equal, so its 1 1/2 in is drawn at the head, and its stair measures must come back not measured (the reader's cross-check), never a value. Step 5 (doors, handrails, guards): houses 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 (house 3 stays step 4: its guard is along a stair, not read yet). A host draws each door with the maker's clear opening the measuring code works out from house.yaml (the less favourable of drawn and stated) confirmed on its type, and none for house 5's sliding door (no clear width worked by hand: its height must come back not measured); and house 4's loft clear of its bedroom (drawn over it, its floor would be the bedroom's ceiling, which the hand-worked house doesn't give). Step 6 (the architect's "this house has none of" ticks) draws the houses with an empty list ticked, and the first real house, `../real_houses/orangetown_ny_80_south_william/`, where its `layout.yaml` places it: the second floor's rooms and walls, the main floor's outline and garage and the basement's outline at the plans' dimension strings, each marked window and the front door in its wall; the main floor's partitions aren't drawn, the floor-to-floor heights, two window sizes, every window's height and the pairs' spacing are assumed (each listed in `layout.yaml` with its effect), and its stairs and guards are placed but not drawn: its rooms, windows and front door are in scope, its seven `has_none` claims are ticked and must come back with what each brings, every clear opening must come back not measured, and `packages/design_checker/tests/test_real_house_revit_reading.py` checks its reading against its `hand_check.yaml`. A host with any recording must have one of it too (`coverage_problems`), and it must pass.

# New York State's Building Code: commercial egress

New York's law for every building its house code does not cover: the **2025 Building Code of New York State** (BCNYS). A state law folder like its sibling [`new_york_state/`](../new_york_state/README.md) (the 2025 Residential Code, for houses): its settings file is `state.yaml`, so it is no place. **The City of Ithaca inherits it** beside the house code (2026-11-22), and a check runs it for every building the Residential Code doesn't cover, as `design_checker/code_scope.py` decides from RCNYS R101.2 ([docs/decisions/2026-11-22_which_ny_code.md](../../../../docs/decisions/2026-11-22_which_ny_code.md)): its rules on the route measuring's stories, spaces and corridors, each whole-building flag its own needs-review line. Why this copy, what is in scope and what is next: [docs/decisions/2026-11-21_ny_building_code.md](../../../../docs/decisions/2026-11-21_ny_building_code.md).

- **Which book covers what.** The 2025 Residential Code of New York State: one- and two-family dwellings and townhouses not more than three stories above grade plane (`new_york_state/`). The 2025 Building Code of New York State: every other building (this folder). Both are in force since **2025-12-31**; between the Notice of Adoption (State Register of 2025-10-01) and that date, a person could comply with either the 2025 or the 2020 Uniform Code (DOS, https://dos.ny.gov/notice-adoption, read 2026-11-21). A project permitted under the 2020 code during that window is not checked by these rules.

## What differs from the 2009 IBC (Maine's)

- **Travel distance (Table 1017.2):** I-4 150 ft without a sprinkler system (the 2009 IBC: not permitted), 200 ft with one, but only per 903.3.1.1 (note c, NFPA 13): an I-4 building with an NFPA 13R system only is held to 150 ft (the stricter reading).
- **Common path:** no section caps it as 2009's 1014.3 did; the 75 ft is in Table 1006.2.1, "spaces with one exit or exit access doorway". Open question 1.
- **Accessory occupancies (508.2.3):** 10 percent of "the floor area of the story" (2009: "the building area of the story"), printed in roman, so not a defined term (201.4); the book defines floor area two ways (202 FLOOR AREA, GROSS and NET). The rule passes only within 10 percent of the net (`story_net_floor_area`), fails only over 10 percent of the gross (`story_gross_floor_area`), and needs review between them (open question 2). Table 506.2's tabular values are a flag of their own.
- **Exit separation on a story (1007.1.1):** a close pair fails only where the story needs two exits: Group I-4 on an upper story, or on the first story above or below grade plane with more than 10 occupants or more than 75 ft of travel (Table 1006.3.4(2)), and the architect states 1006.3.4 item 2 (rooms that exit directly outside at the level of exit discharge, on any story) is not relied on (`story_rooms_exit_directly_outside`: no); where that fact is blank or yes, where one exit may do, or in another or unstated occupancy, it needs review. Occupant-load bands are read as the tables print them, on the safe side: two exits up to 500 (Table 1006.3.3's '1-500'), so 500.5 needs three.
- **Exit separation in a space (1007.1.1, 1007.1.2):** a close pair fails only where the encoded I-4 row of Table 1006.2.1 requires two (or 1006.2.1.1 three, from 501 occupants); in another occupancy, an unstated one, or a space that may have fewer, it needs review.
- **Egress through an elevator lobby (1016.2 item 4):** a lobby that can be locked is a locked room (fails) unless its exit access door is electrically locked per 1010.2.14 (its own flag), which needs review (`space_egress_through_electrically_locked_lobby`).
- **Dead ends (1020.5):** as 2009's 1018.4 for I-4: 20 ft, or any length under 2.5 times the least width.

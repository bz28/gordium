# New York State's Building Code: commercial egress

New York's law for every building its house code does not cover: the **2025 Building Code of New York State** (BCNYS). A state law folder like its sibling [`new_york_state/`](../new_york_state/README.md) (the 2025 Residential Code, for houses): its settings file is `state.yaml`, so it is no place. **No place inherits it yet**, so no design check runs these rules today (wiring it into Check project is a later PR: a check must first know whether a building is a house or not, below). Why this copy, what is in scope and what is next: [docs/decisions/2026-11-21_ny_building_code.md](../../../../docs/decisions/2026-11-21_ny_building_code.md).

- **Which book covers what.** The 2025 Residential Code of New York State: one- and two-family dwellings and townhouses not more than three stories above grade plane (`new_york_state/`). The 2025 Building Code of New York State: every other building (this folder). Both are in force since **2025-12-31**; between the Notice of Adoption (State Register of 2025-10-01) and that date, a person could comply with either the 2025 or the 2020 Uniform Code (DOS, https://dos.ny.gov/notice-adoption, read 2026-11-21). A project permitted under the 2020 code during that window is not checked by these rules.
- **The copy:** Public.Resource.Org's on the Internet Archive (`gov.ny.building.2025`, posted 2026-02-02; Ben chose such copies for now, 2026-11-21): ICC's own PDF, real text, not a scan, kept in the code cache outside the repo (copyright: only short exact quotes here). It carries an ICC logo and a per-copy number, 102102166, at the top of every page (the house code's copy carries the same), which is page furniture, never quoted. **Verified against this copy only; check against a copy bought from ICC.**
- **Brought in** (`1_source_documents/sources.yaml`): 308.5 (Group I-4, day care), 508.2 (accessory occupancies), 903.3.1.1 and 903.3.1.2 (the two sprinkler systems the limits name, without the parts under them), 1006.2.1 and Table 1006.2.1 (common path; spaces with one exit), 1007.1 (exit separation), 1016.2 (egress through intervening spaces), Section 1017 and Table 1017.2 (exit access travel distance), 1020.5 (dead ends), 201 and every Section 202 definition they use (by the book's italics, 201.1): 148 pieces.
- **How it is read:** ICC's PDF, line by line (`layout: icc`, `style: bcnys_2025`: `document_extraction/icc_sections.py`). Tables are pieces of their own, their lines named in `sources.yaml`. The plain text runs a table's raised note letter into its cell ("200c" for 200, note c); each is put right by line (`brought_in.misread`), checked by position (the letter is 5.04 points beside 8.4-point digits) and on the page image.
- **`text_verification.yaml`:** every number of every piece read a second time from the page images by helpers that never saw the extracted text (all 148 pieces, 296 numbers, agree), the two tables also cell by cell by position, and each number's comparison read a third way from the whole sentence; where the recorded words miss it (a table cell, "fewer than 24 hours"), a flag says which is right. A table prints "(feet)" only in its column headings: its distance cells are lengths in feet because their flag says so (`code_numbers._column_feet`); its occupant-load cells stay counts.
- **Rules:** `5_building_code_rules/drafted/general.yaml`, 28 hand-drafted (drafts: approval is deferred), for Group I-4 first: 8 rules (travel distance, a space's two exits, three or four exits, exit separation, three or more exits, intervening spaces, dead ends, 10 percent accessory area) and 20 flags (classification, allowances, the tables' other rows, what is not measured yet). The building's facts are proposed measures (`drafted/records/proposed_measures.yaml`: occupancy group, sprinkler system, other occupancies, a balcony increase, an atrium). On the Maine sheet's own numbers (the same day care as if built in New York: `rule_format/tests/test_ny_building_egress.py`), every check passes, as it did for the sheet: travel 130'-8" (200 ft, NFPA 13), common path 48'-6" (75 ft), dead end 21'-6" (under 2.5 x 10 ft), remoteness 0.8745 (one-third), storage 1,428 sf (10 percent of 16,509 sf).
- **Not checked yet:** none (`6_coverage_report/gov.ny.building.2025.rules.yaml`).

## What differs from the 2009 IBC (Maine's)

- **Travel distance (Table 1017.2):** I-4 150 ft without a sprinkler system (the 2009 IBC: not permitted), 200 ft with one, but only per 903.3.1.1 (note c, NFPA 13): an I-4 building with an NFPA 13R system only is held to 150 ft (the stricter reading).
- **Common path:** no section caps it as 2009's 1014.3 did; the 75 ft is in Table 1006.2.1, "spaces with one exit or exit access doorway". Open question 1.
- **Accessory occupancies (508.2.3):** 10 percent of "the floor area of the story" (2009: "the building area of the story"). Gordium's reading, for the architects to confirm: the story's gross floor area (`story_gross_floor_area`); "floor area" is printed in roman, so it is not a defined term (201.4). Table 506.2's tabular values are a flag of their own.
- **Dead ends (1020.5):** as 2009's 1018.4 for I-4: 20 ft, or any length under 2.5 times the least width.

## Open questions (for the architects or the Department of State)

1. **Does Table 1006.2.1 cap a story's common path, or only decide when a space needs a second exit?** 1006.2.1 reads as a trigger ("two exits ... shall be provided where ... the common path ... exceeds the values"); the 2009 IBC's 1014.3 was a cap. ICC's commentary may not be read by a script; no New York interpretation was found. Until answered, a space with two exits and a common path over 75 ft needs review, never passes (`general.bc_1006_2_1_two_exits`).
2. **508.2.3's "floor area of the story":** gross (Gordium's reading) or another measure.

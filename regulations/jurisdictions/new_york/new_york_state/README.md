# New York State's own law

Law that every New York place inherits (a place lists this folder in its `jurisdiction.yaml` under `inherits:`; Ithaca does). For now the link is declared and checked; nothing reads this folder's law into a place's check yet, since there are no egress rules yet. It is not a place: its settings file is `state.yaml`, not `jurisdiction.yaml`, so the place registry and the Rhino plugin never treat it as one. Why, and how: [docs/decisions/2026-11-12_building_code_text.md](../../../../docs/decisions/2026-11-12_building_code_text.md).

- **The book:** the 2025 Residential Code of New York State (one- and two-family houses and townhouses up to three stories), in force since 2025-12-31.
- **Brought in so far:**
  - *Egress step 1:* R318 means of egress, R319 emergency escape and rescue openings, R320 handrails, R321 guards and window fall protection, R325.6 and R325.7 (stairway lighting, which R318.7.10 requires), R201 (how definitions apply), and every Section R202 definition those use.
  - *Egress step 1b (the parts the egress text points to):* R314 mezzanines, R315 sleeping lofts, R316 habitable attics (each whole: their size limits decide whether the space is a mezzanine, a loft or a story, and so which egress limits apply), R507.10 deck guards; R101.2.1 (which appendices are law) and R102.6 (work on existing buildings goes to Appendix BO); and the parts of **Appendix BO (existing buildings)** that set egress for work on existing one- and two-family houses (below). Appendix BO's own definitions (Section BO103) apply in Appendix BO only.
  - *Egress step 1c:* **Appendix BF (patio covers)**: BF101 (scope and permitted uses), BF102 (its definition, PATIO COVER, which applies in Appendix BF only) and BF103 (a patio cover's walls and openings, and BF103.2: an escape opening from a sleeping room into a patio cover, and an exit into one). Below.
  - No rules yet.
- **Copyright:** the book is ICC's. The repo keeps only short exact quotes with their section, page and a link; the book and its text are in the code cache outside the repo (`1_source_documents/sources.yaml` says where and why). Where the cache is missing (CI), the checks that need the text are skipped and say so.
- **`text_verification.yaml`:** every number in the brought-in pieces, read a second time from the page images by a reader who never saw the extracted text, and whether the two readings agree (all 286 pieces agree); and what the printed sentence says of each number (at least, at most, more than, less than, a range, or "none": no comparison on that number itself, as for a conversion in brackets, a count or the 4-inch sphere an opening must not pass, whose limit is on the opening; such limits are in the labels and quotes, not here), read by a helper from the whole sentence without the code that reads the numbers, checked by code against the recorded words. The 33 numbers where they differ each carry a flag saying which is right (most are limits printed further back, "a minimum tread depth of 5 inches", which the recorded words miss; one, R315.2's "more than one-half", is the law backwards). Everything is **verified against the Public.Resource.Org copy only; check against an exact copy bought from ICC**.

See `regulations/README.md` for what each numbered step means. There is no `5_zoning_rules/` yet: egress rules come in a later step.

## Appendix BO: adopted, and which parts are brought in

**Appendix BO is law in New York, not an optional appendix.** The book says so twice: R101.2.1 ("Provisions in the following appendices have been adopted and are made part of this code: ... Appendix BO Existing Buildings and Structures", p.24, lines 38-42, brought in as a piece) and the preface ("The provisions of Appendix BO have been adopted and are part of this code", p.14, line 4). The State's rules incorporate the 2025 RCNYS whole (19 NYCRR §1220.2(b)) and amend Appendix BO in one place (§1220.2(e)(4), below), which they would not do to an appendix that was not law. R102.6.1 and R102.6.2 send additions, alterations, repairs and changes of use to it. (Appendices BE, BG to BN and CA to CG are "included for informational purposes" only, R101.2.1.)

Brought in (egress for work on existing one- and two-family houses, and the parts that say when they apply): BO101 (purpose: new-construction rules apply except as the appendix modifies them), BO102.1 with BO102.1.2 (no work may make the building less compliant, and nothing in the appendix lets a feature built in conflict with the codes stay: this limits every existing-condition allowance below), BO102.6 with BO102.6.3 (replacement windows: window fall protection), BO103 (its definitions, as used), BO104.1 and BO104.1.1 (repairs: new-construction rules, and the existing level of life safety kept), BO105.1 (Level 1 and Level 2 alterations), BO105.2.2 and BO105.2.3 (replacement doors and escape windows), BO105.3 (nonconformities), BO105.8 (means of egress), BO105.10 (stairs, handrails and guards), BO105.11 (escape openings in reconfigured basements), BO105.13 (habitable attics above the second story), BO106.1 and BO106.7 (additions; the sprinkler exception that needs escape openings), BO107.1, BO107.2, BO107.2.1 and BO107.2.2 (change of occupancy: existing escape openings and stairs), BO108 (bed and breakfast conversions), BO109.1, BO109.8, BO109.8.1, BO109.8.5 and BO109.8.6 (historic buildings: egress widths, stair railings, guard openings). BO102.1, BO102.6, BO106.7, BO107.2 and BO109.8 are brought in without the parts under them that are not egress (`brought_in.alone`). Everything else in the appendix is not brought in, by kind: structure (BO104.5, BO105.4, BO106.2 to BO106.4, BO107.2.4, BO107.2.5, BO109.9 with BO109.9.1, dangerous conditions), energy (BO102.6.1, BO102.13), materials and the rest of repairs (BO104.2 to BO104.4, BO104.6 to BO104.9: materials, water closets, electrical, mechanical, safety glazing, reroofing, plumbing), safety glazing and reroofing in alterations (BO102.6.2, BO105.2.4), plumbing, electrical and mechanical (BO105.5 to BO105.7, BO106.9 to BO106.11), ceiling heights (BO105.9, BO107.2.3), fire protection (BO105.2.5 fire separation, BO105.2.1, BO105.2.6 and BO109.8.2 finishes, BO109.8.3 fire-resistant assemblies, BO109.8.4 a stairway enclosure's smoke-tight doors, which sets no width or height, BO106.7.1 and BO107.2.6 sprinklers), smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, flood hazard areas (BO102.7, BO106.8, BO109.3), relocated buildings (BO109.14, BO110) and replacement buildings, posted notices (the bed-and-breakfast evacuation notice of BO102.10, an operating matter like R318.1.1), and these, which hold no egress limit of their own: BO102.1.1 (existing installations may stay: an allowance, so leaving it out only makes Gordium stricter), BO102.1.3 (corrections other codes require), BO102.2 to BO102.5 and BO102.7 to BO102.13 (alterations' general provisions: other codes, home occupations, lead paint, oil tanks, grease interceptors and the like), BO105.12 and BO106.6 (fuel-burning appliances), BO106.5 (manufactured homes), BO109.13 (light, ventilation and heating), BO105.2 itself (newly built elements follow the code; its two exceptions are about light and electrical work, and its egress parts BO105.2.2 and BO105.2.3 are brought in), BO109.2 (a historic building's written report), BO109.4 (unsafe conditions), and BO109.10 to BO109.12 (a historic building's repairs, alterations and change of occupancy follow BO104, BO105 and BO107, whose egress parts are brought in; what BO109 permits instead is in the brought-in BO109.8 parts).

Every allowance for an existing condition (an existing stair's width, headroom, landings, riser and tread; existing escape openings; historic stairs and guards) is a **flag**, never a rule: whether a stair already exists, whether it was lawful when built (BO102.1.2), and the class of the work are the architect's input, and the model can't show them. So these looser limits never relax R318 for a new stair.

What the appendix's text gets wrong or leaves open (each is in the labels, as a flag, so it shows as needing review):

- **BO108.4 item 2** (bed and breakfast exterior stair) says "conforming to the requirements of R311.7". In this book R311 is carbon monoxide alarms and has no R311.7; R311.7 is the model code's (the IRC's) number for stairways, which this book renumbers to R318.7. Gordium's reading: R318.7 is meant (strong common practice: the appendix is ICC's model text with New York's numbers not carried through). A reading for the architects to confirm, not law.
- **BO107.2** sends a two-family-to-one-family change's smoke and carbon monoxide alarms to "Section BO109", which is historic buildings and holds no alarm rule (BO102.4 and BO102.5 do). Not egress; noted only.
- **BO105.8** sends every alteration's means of egress to Section 702 of the Property Maintenance Code of New York State, another code, not brought in: an alteration's egress needs review for that part.
- **The book's own misnumbering**, cut where printed and named in `sources.yaml` (`printed_out_of_order`): Section BO109 goes from BO109.4 to BO109.8 (no BO109.5 to BO109.7; p.973, line 54), and prints a "BO112.12.1" between BO109.12 and BO109.12.1 (p.974, line 39). BO105.1 and BO104.1 also print "Section B0109" (a zero for the letter O; p.968, line 13 and p.967, line 16).
- **New York's amendment to the appendix:** 19 NYCRR §1220.2(e)(4) changes the second definition printed "HOME OCCUPATION" in BO103.1 (p.966, line 38) to "LOAD-BEARING ELEMENT", with the same words. The cutter cuts it under the law's term (`amended_by_law`). It is not used by any brought-in part.
- **Ceiling heights in BO105.9 and BO107.2.3** (not brought in: not egress) print "6 feet 8 inches (2134 mm)" in their exception 1; 6 feet 8 inches is 2032 mm (2134 mm is 7 feet). Noted for when those come in.

New York amends the book in three other places (19 NYCRR §1220.2(e)(1)-(3)): PERMIT (a brought-in definition: the amendment gives the same words, tagged [NY]; it may also drop the "[NY] Building permit" entry printed under it; PERMIT sets no egress requirement), referenced standards (Chapter 44) and BA113.3 (manufactured homes). None changes an egress limit.

## Appendix BF (patio covers): adopted, and which parts are brought in

**Appendix BF is law in New York, not an optional appendix.** R101.2.1 lists it among the appendices that "have been adopted and are made part of this code" ("Appendix BF Patio Covers", p.24, line 41, brought in as a piece), and the preface says so too ("The provisions of Appendix BF have been adopted and are part of this code", p.13, line 13). The State's rules incorporate the 2025 RCNYS whole (19 NYCRR §1220.2(b)); of their four amendments to the book (§1220.2(e)(1)-(4): PERMIT, the referenced standards, BA113.3 and BO103.1), none touches Appendix BF (DOS rule text of 2025-12-02, pp.6-9, read again 2026-11-12, same sha256 as in `sources.yaml`). So Appendix BF is read as printed.

Brought in: BF101 (BF101.1 scope: a patio cover meets BF101 to BF106; BF101.2 permitted uses: recreational, outdoor living, never a carport, garage, storage room or habitable room), BF102 (BF102.1 and PATIO COVER: "A structure with open or glazed walls that is used for recreational, outdoor living purposes associated with a dwelling unit"), BF103 (BF103.1 enclosure walls: the longer wall and one other at least 65 percent open or glazed below 6 feet 8 inches, the openings filled with insect screening, approved plastic not more than 0.125 inch thick, or glass; BF103.2, below), and R202's VENTILATION, which BF103.2 prints in italics. All on p.898. Not brought in, being no egress: BF104 (height: one story, not more than 12 feet), BF105 (BF105.1 design loads, BF105.2 footings), BF106 (aluminum screen enclosures in hurricane-prone regions: BF106.1 to BF106.5, with the definition SCREEN ENCLOSURE under BF106.2, its wind figure and tables on pp.899-900). BF101.1 points to them, so it is a flag: where a design has a patio cover, those parts need review.

**BF103.2** (labeled a rule) says: openings required for light and ventilation into a patio structure "shall be unenclosed where such openings serve as emergency egress or rescue openings from sleeping rooms"; and where such an opening is an exit from the dwelling unit, "the patio structure, unless unenclosed, shall be provided with exits conforming to the provisions of Section R318" (brought in). Two words are not defined. For "patio structure" the label's notes give Gordium's reading, for the architects (or the City) to confirm before a rule is drafted; "unenclosed" is an open question:

- **"patio structure"** is a patio cover: BF103.2 ties it to "Section BF101", which governs patio covers.
- **"unenclosed"** is not settled. One reading: nothing may fill the patio cover's wall openings, since BF103.1 says a patio cover's openings are "enclosed with" insect screening, plastic or glass (so a screened porch is enclosed); others read insect screening, or any patio cover open or glazed enough to meet BF103.1, as unenclosed. Searched (2026-11-12): the whole appendix (BF103.1 is its only other use of "enclosed"); R319 (an escape opening opens to a public way, yard or court); the model code's same words (the International Residential Code's Appendix AH103.2, which names R311 where this book names R318) as other places adopt it (the City of Houston's, on UpCodes, https://up.codes/viewer/houston/irc-2021/chapter/AH/patio-covers, read 2026-11-12): no commentary or interpretation of "unenclosed" shown, and no Ithaca or New York interpretation found. ICC's commentary is not read (its site may not be read by any script or automated browser). **Open question for the architects or the City**, recorded in the label's `open_questions` with the readings still in play (so it shows in the review list and blocks approval). Every reading still in play is measured: a design passes only if it passes under all of them, fails only if it fails under all of them, and otherwise needs review. Until the question is answered, a bedroom's escape window into a screened, plastic or glazed patio cover needs review, never a pass; only one with nothing in its openings passes.

Whether an opening is a sleeping room's escape opening or an exit door, whether it opens into a patio cover, and what fills the patio cover's openings must be in the model; where any is not, the result is needs review.

How the cutter reads Appendix BF: its section of definitions is titled "DEFINITION" (singular), and BF106.2, a numbered part titled "Definition", prints a second term (SCREEN ENCLOSURE) inside Section BF106; the cutter reads both (`document_extraction/icc_sections.py`), and a term under a part is named by that part (`BF106.2:screen_enclosure`), so the part saying where it applies comes in with it, never the rest of its section.

## What the book's text gets wrong

- **R315.5.2.1's metric conversion** (p.98, line 29): "20 inches (610 mm)"; 20 inches is 508 mm, and 610 mm is 24 inches. The same width prints "20 inches (508 mm)" in R318.7.11.1 (p.102, line 7) and in Appendix BB's loft stairs (BB104.2.1.1, p.881). Gordium's reading: 20 inches governs; a reading for the architects to confirm. The label's notes say so.
- **R318.7.1's metric conversions** (p.100, line 25): "31 1/2 inches (787 mm)" and "27 inches (698 mm)"; 31 1/2 inches is 800 mm and 27 inches is 686 mm. Gordium's reading: the inches govern, the millimetres being a conversion printed after them; a reading for the architects to confirm before a rule is drafted from these widths. The label's notes say so, and a test (`rule_format/tests/test_state_law.py`, where the code cache is) finds every "inches (mm)" pair in the brought-in pieces off by more than the book's rounding (2 mm, or 1% of a larger length) and requires its label's notes to name it; it needs the code cache, so CI skips it.
- **R318.7.5.1's riser slope** (p.100, line 45): "30 degrees (0.51 rad)"; 30 degrees is 0.52 rad. **R319.4.1's area well** (p.103, line 10): "9 square feet (0.9 m²)"; 9 square feet is 0.84 m². The same reading: the US units govern; each label's notes say so, and the same test finds every "N degrees (rad)", "N square feet (m²)" and "N inches (cm)" pair that does not convert at the precision printed ("200 gross square feet (18.6 m²)", R318.7.12 and R318.7.13, is not looked at; it converts).

## Ways into these sections from outside them

Found by the same code: the whole book (all 1,079 pages, the code cache's text of every page, read by the same extractor: `document_extraction/book_text.py`) searched for every brought-in section outside the brought-in pieces, leaving out the pages that only list section numbers (found by their running head: the contents, pp.16-22; the referenced standards, pp.833-866; the index, pp.1070-1076). Step 1b brought in R314.4 mezzanines, R315.5 sleeping lofts, R316.4 habitable attics and R507.10 deck guards (each with its section), and Appendix BO's egress parts. These are still not brought in:

- R301.2.2.7 height limitations (p.75): a mezzanine that complies with R314 is not counted as a story (a seismic and structural limit).
- R324.4.7 glazing beside the bottom stair landing (p.107): an exception for glass protected by a guard that meets R321.
- R502.3.1 floor joists (p.166): attics reached by a fixed stairway per R318.7 (a structural load matter).
- M1602.1 outdoor air openings (p.529): refers to R325.6.
- Appendix BB tiny houses (pp.880-881): loft stairs, landings, handrails, ladders, guards and escape openings, modifying R318-R321. Left for later (Ben, 2026-11-12).
- R325.1.1 Exception 1 and R325.1.2 Exception 3 (pp.108-109): a habitable room's required light and ventilation openings may face into a roofed porch, deck or patio that is open enough (65 percent of its longer side unobstructed and a ceiling at least 7 feet high for light; 40 percent of its perimeter open for ventilation). Light and ventilation, not egress; BF103.2 (brought in) sets what applies where such an opening is also an escape opening or an exit.
- Appendix BO's own other parts that point to R102.6.1 (structure: BO102.3, BO104.5, BO105.4) and to R319 (BO106.7, brought in).

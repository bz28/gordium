## Causes, each resolved by reading the law

- **`bf103_2_every_exit_door`** (8 disagreements), **drafts_stricter**: The drafts flag every exit door for BF103.2's second sentence (a patio cover an exit opens into must have R318 exits unless unenclosed). No measure says whether an exit door opens into a patio cover, so every house needs review; and where an example names no exit door at all, the flag's kind is unread, so the building needs review. The key drafts only the first sentence (escape openings), where opening_into_patio_cover exists. A measure for exit doors is a to-do. Law: BF103.2: "Where such exterior openings serve as an exit from the dwelling unit, the patio structure, unless unenclosed, shall be provided with exits conforming to the provisions of Section R318."
- **`bf103_2_open_patio_cover`** (1 disagreement), **drafts_stricter**: An escape opening into a patio cover whose wall openings hold nothing (patio_cover_openings_open) is into an unenclosed structure: the law asks no more (key: not applicable). The drafts' flag ignores that measure and needs review. The open question (does screening make it enclosed) only arises when something fills them. Law: BF103.2: "Exterior openings required for light and ventilation into a patio structure conforming to Section BF101 shall be unenclosed where such openings serve as emergency egress or rescue openings from sleeping rooms."
- **`handrail_examples_without_a_stair`** (17 disagreements), **both_follow_the_law**: The key's handrail rules group handrails by the stair they sit in (parts.yaml: a handrail sits within a stair); the drafts check every handrail. The drafts' examples give handrails with no stair, which the key's rows read as unplaced (needs review), and the key's examples give a stair with no handrail, a "none" row the drafts don't have. On a model where each handrail sits in its stair, both give the same answers. Law: R320.2: "Handrail height ... shall be not less than 34 inches (864 mm) and not more than 38 inches (965 mm)"; R320.4: "Handrails adjacent to a wall shall have a space of not less than 1 1/2 inches (38 mm) between the wall and the handrails."
- **`loft_stair_may_follow_r318_7`** (11 disagreements), **drafts_stricter**: A sleeping loft's stair "shall be permitted to comply" with R315.5.2.1 to R315.5.2.3: a choice, so it may meet either those or R318.7's ordinary limits. The key leaves loft stairs out of R318.7.1/.2/.5 (R315.5.2's limits are looser, so meeting R318.7's meets them) and passes a loft stair that meets R318.7.5 under R315.5.2.2. The drafts apply R318.7.5.1 to loft stairs (fail a 9 in riser R315.5.2.2 allows), make loft stairs need review under R318.7.1/.2/.5.2, and apply R315.5.2.2 alone (fail a 6 1/2 in riser on an ordinary stair; flag the formula on every loft stair). Law: R315.5.2: "Stairways providing egress from sleeping lofts shall be permitted to comply with Sections R315.5.2.1 through R315.5.2.3."
- **`missing_input_in_the_example`** (2 disagreements), **both_follow_the_law**: The example leaves out a measure one side's rule reads: the side whose rule needs it gives needs review, the other a definite answer that the law supports (a 35.5 in stair width fails R318.7.1 whatever the width at the handrail; a loft stair whose shortest riser is 6 in fails R315.5.2.2 and, at 2 in variation, R318.7.5.1). Law: R318.7.1: "Stairways shall be not less than 36 inches (914 mm) in clear width at all points above the permitted handrail height"; R315.5.2.2: "Risers ... shall be not less than 7 inches (178 mm)".
- **`pass_or_not_applicable`** (1 disagreement), **both_follow_the_law**: A 44 in deep area well needs no ladder: the key says pass (its check holds), the drafts not applicable (their applies_when is "deeper than 44 in"). The same answer in law. Law: R319.4.2: "Area wells with a vertical depth greater than 44 inches (1118 mm) shall be equipped with ... ladder or steps."
- **`r315_5_2_1_width_at_the_handrail`** (2 disagreements), **drafts_stricter**: stair_clear_width_at_handrail is the least width "at and below" the handrail (parts.yaml, R318.7.1's split), but R315.5.2.1 puts "at" the handrail with the 17 in limit and only "below" with 20 in. A loft stair measuring 17 to 20 in there may be lawful (the narrow point may be at the handrail): the key needs review, the drafts fail. Law: R315.5.2.1: "Stairways providing egress from a sleeping loft shall not be less than 17 inches (432 mm) in clear width at or above the handrail. The width below the handrail shall be not less than 20 inches (610 mm)."
- **`r318_2_doors_counted`** (18 disagreements), **draft_wrong** (could pass what the law fails): The drafts check "one egress door for each dwelling unit" by counting the lot's egress doors against its dwelling units (a proposed building measure, egress_doors). A two-family house whose two marked doors both serve one unit would pass. The key checks every marked door, fails a house with none marked, and makes a house with more than one unit need review (which door serves which unit isn't in the model). Most of these rows are the drafts' count rule needing review because the examples don't give egress_doors. Law: R318.2: "Not less than one egress door shall be provided for each dwelling unit."
- **`r318_2_readily_openable_flag`** (1 disagreement), **drafts_stricter**: The drafts flag every egress door for "readily openable from inside without a key", so every house needs review. That is hardware, which a design model doesn't show; the key leaves it out. Neither passes it. Law: R318.2: "Egress doors shall be readily openable from inside the dwelling unit without the use of a key or special knowledge or effort."
- **`r318_7_8_spiral_review`** (1 disagreement), **drafts_stricter**: Spiral stairs need a handrail like any flight of four or more risers (R318.7.11 keeps R318.7, R318.7.11.1 says nothing of handrails); the drafts make them need review, the key passes one with a handrail. Law: R318.7.8: "Handrails shall be provided on not less than one side of each flight of stairs with four or more risers."
- **`r319_1_none_marked`** (2 disagreements), **draft_wrong**: The drafts make a sleeping room with no marked escape opening need review; Gordium's decision (Ben, 2026-11-12, for the architects to confirm) is that it fails, as the key does. Exception 3 (a sprinklered house's basement bedrooms) is the case review would catch; it is the architect's to claim. Law: R319.1: "Basements, habitable attics, the room to which a sleeping loft is open, and every sleeping room shall have not less than one operable emergency escape and rescue opening."
- **`r319_1_room_a_loft_opens_to`** (1 disagreement), **draft_wrong** (could pass what the law fails): R319.1 also requires an escape opening in "the room to which a sleeping loft is open". The drafts have no rule for it, so a loft opening onto a room that isn't a bedroom, basement or attic, with no escape opening, is never flagged. The key flags each sleeping loft (no part says which room it opens to). Law: R319.1: "Basements, habitable attics, the room to which a sleeping loft is open, and every sleeping room shall have not less than one operable emergency escape and rescue opening."
- **`r319_1_yard_review`** (4 disagreements), **drafts_stricter**: The drafts make an opening that doesn't open to a yard or public way need review (for exception 4); the measure opening_opens_to_yard already includes exception 4's 36 in path (parts.yaml, key fix above), so an opening marked "no" fails, as the key says. Law: R319.1: "Emergency escape and rescue openings shall open directly into a public way, or to a yard or court that opens to a public way."
- **`r319_4_1_full_opening_flag`** (9 disagreements), **drafts_stricter**: The drafts flag "the area well lets the opening fully open" on every below-grade opening, though the measure opening_fully_openable exists: every such opening needs review, and one that can't fully open is not failed. The key fails it. (Where a drafts' example leaves opening_fully_openable out, the comparison reads it as no, as it does every left-out yes/no: book_eval.completed.) Law: R319.4.1: "The size of the area well shall allow the emergency escape and rescue opening to be fully opened."
- **`r319_4_1_ladder_review_too_wide`** (1 disagreement), **drafts_stricter**: The ladder may encroach at most 6 in into the area well's required dimensions, so a 24 in projection fails even with a ladder (30 in is the least). The drafts need review for any well with a ladder under 36 in; the key fails under 30 in and needs review from 30 to 36 in. Law: R319.4.1 exception: "The ladder or steps required by Section R319.4.2 shall be permitted to encroach not more than 6 inches (152 mm) into the required dimensions of the area well."
- **`r319_4_2_not_tied_to_below_grade`** (3 disagreements), **drafts_stricter**: Area wells are for openings whose sill is below grade (R319.4); the drafts' ladder rule doesn't test opening_below_grade, so an opening above grade needs review for a missing well depth, or is checked on a depth it has no well for. Never a pass the law denies. Law: R319.4: "An emergency escape and rescue opening where the bottom of the clear opening is below the adjacent grade shall be provided with an area well"; R319.4.2: "Area wells with a vertical depth greater than 44 inches (1118 mm) shall be equipped with an approved, permanently affixed ladder or steps."
- **`r320_2_whole_rail_too_high`** (1 disagreement), **draft_wrong**: Exception 2 lets the handrail rise above 38 in only at fittings and bendings. A rail whose lowest point is 40 in is too high along its whole length: the key fails it; the drafts' review (any rail over 38 in somewhere and 34 in at least) needs review instead. Law: R320.2: "... shall be not less than 34 inches (864 mm) and not more than 38 inches (965 mm)." Exception 2: "... the handrail height at the fittings or bendings shall be permitted to exceed 38 inches (965 mm)."
- **`r321_1_required_guards_only`** (9 disagreements), **drafts_stricter**: R321.1.2 and R321.1.3 limit "required guards": those R321.1.1 requires, where the drop is more than 30 in. The drafts check every guard present, so an optional guard on a 24 in drop fails or needs review. The key tests the drop; where an example leaves the drop out, the key needs review and the drafts answer by the guard alone (a pass there is right whatever the drop: the guard meets the limit). Law: R321.1.2: "Required guards at open-sided walking surfaces ... shall be not less than 36 inches (914 mm) in height"; R321.1.1: "... located more than 30 inches (762 mm) measured vertically to the floor or grade below".
- **`spiral_stair_left_out`** (5 disagreements), **draft_wrong** (could pass what the law fails): The drafts leave spiral stairs out of the riser and tread variation limits. A spiral stair still meets R318.7 except where R318.7.11.1 says otherwise, and R318.7.11.1 makes its treads identical (stricter than 3/8 in); the exceptions send only the riser height and the opening between treads elsewhere. So a spiral stair with treads 1 in apart is unlawful and the drafts say not applicable; the key fails it (treads) or needs review (risers). Law: R318.7.11: "Spiral stairways ... shall comply with the requirements of Section R318.7 except as specified in Sections R318.7.11.1 and R318.7.11.2."; R318.7.11.1: "Treads shall be identical, and the rise shall be not more than 9 1/2 inches (241 mm)."
